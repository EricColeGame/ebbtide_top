## Why the Ebbtide Little Bird Helicopter Could Define Air Play

Los Angeles is overrun, the rooftops are the only clean ground left, and the fastest way to move a fireteam between them is the smallest airframe on the map. The **Ebbtide Little Bird helicopter** fills exactly that niche: a light, fast rotorcraft built for rooftop insertions, civilian pull-outs, and hit-and-run fire support in a game that blends real-time strategy command with hands-on survival. If you plan to hold a city against a horde, knowing what the **Ebbtide Little Bird helicopter** can and cannot survive is the difference between a clean extraction and a very expensive crash site.

One important caveat before we go deeper: Ebbtide is still in development, and the studio has been explicit that visuals and features shown in previews may change. Nothing here is a final stat sheet. What follows combines what community showcases have actually demonstrated, real-world reference data on the MH-6/AH-6 Little Bird family, and patterns that hold true across almost every RTS that fields light helicopters.

## Real-World Little Bird vs. Its Likely In-Game Role

The Little Bird name comes from the MD 500 family, flown most famously by the U.S. Army's 160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment in both MH-6 (transport) and AH-6 (attack) configurations. Those real-world characteristics map almost perfectly onto the role a zombie RTS needs filled.

| Real-world reference point | Typical figure | Likely Ebbtide translation |
|---|---|---|
| Crew | 2 (pilot plus co-pilot/gunner) | Small crew requirement, cheap to field and replace |
| Troop capacity | Up to 6 on external bench seats | Squad-sized lifts — enough for a fireteam, not a platoon |
| Top speed | Roughly 280 km/h (about 175 mph) | Fastest responder to a collapsing rooftop or a downed squad |
| Armament (AH-6) | 7.62mm miniguns, .50 cal, 2.75-inch rocket pods | Anti-infantry strafing plus limited anti-vehicle punch |
| Protection | Minimal — light armor at best | Highly vulnerable to sustained ground fire; speed *is* the armor |
| Signature use | Night raids, precision insertion, overwatch | Recon, extraction, and emergency fire support |

The takeaway: expect the Ebbtide Little Bird helicopter to be the unit you call when speed matters more than survivability. It will not soak damage the way an Abrams does. It will get a squad onto a roof, out of a hot zone, or behind a horde in a way nothing else can.

## How to Use the Ebbtide Little Bird Helicopter: Roles and Tactics

Light helicopters fail in RTS games for one reason — commanders use them as gunships. The Little Bird is a utility platform first and an attack platform second. Build your orders around that hierarchy.

| Mission role | Suitability | How to run it |
|---|---|---|
| Squad insertion on rooftops | Excellent | Drop fast, leave faster; never idle on the pad |
| Civilian / survivor extraction | Excellent | Pre-clear the landing zone before the aircraft arrives |
| Recon and map vision | Excellent | Fly the perimeter, hug building lines, avoid open sky |
| Anti-infantry strafing | Good | Short passes only; break contact before the horde clusters |
| Anti-vehicle work | Situational | Rocket runs on isolated targets, never on escorted columns |
| Frontline brawling | Poor | Ground fire and collision risk make it a wasted asset |
| Cargo or heavy lift | Not its job | Use larger airframes for bulk logistics |

Loadout decisions should follow the same logic. An unarmed or door-gunner transport configuration is almost always more valuable than a rocket-heavy build, because the helicopter's real product is *positioning*, not damage.

- **Fly the route before you need it.** Trace your insertion and exfil corridors early, while the sky is quiet.
- **Stage from a forward operating base.** Short hops mean more sorties per fuel load and less exposure.
- **Pair with artillery timing.** Send the Little Bird in the moment your barrage lifts, not during it.
- **Keep a spare airframe.** Attrition is guaranteed; plan replacements into your build order.
- **Use altitude as a shield.** Rooftop-level flight keeps you below the sightlines of most ground threats.

## Base Building, Evacuations, and Keeping It Alive

Because Ebbtide emphasizes base building and forward operating bases, air power is only as good as the infrastructure behind it. A helicopter without a nearby pad, fuel, and repair capacity becomes a one-way trip. Treat aviation as a supply chain problem first.

| Threat | Why it hurts the Ebbtide Little Bird helicopter | Counter |
|---|---|---|
| Massed undead on rooftops | Contact damage and blocked landing zones | Scout the pad with infantry before committing |
| Sustained small-arms fire | Minimal armor means rapid system damage | Fly unpredictable approach angles at speed |
| Obscured or smoke-filled LZ | Aborted approaches burn fuel and time | Establish a secondary pad before every mission |
| Fuel and loiter limits | Forces early withdrawal mid-mission | Stage forward; keep sorties short |
| Building collisions | Instant loss of a valuable asset | Learn the skyline; never fly blind at low level |
| Artillery timing conflicts | Friendly fire on your own corridor | Deconflict fire missions before launch |

A pre-flight routine helps more than any single tactic:

1. Confirm the landing zone is clear and within fuel range.
2. Assign a ground escort to hold the pad while you load or unload.
3. Set the exfil route *before* touching down.
4. Queue repair and refuel so the aircraft is mission-ready on return.
5. Log losses and adjust production — if you are losing an airframe per mission, your approach angles are the problem, not the unit.

## FAQ: Ebbtide Little Bird Helicopter Questions

**Is the Ebbtide Little Bird helicopter confirmed as a playable unit?**
Not officially. Helicopters are confirmed as commandable assets in Ebbtide showcases, but the specific Little Bird airframe has not been formally detailed by the developers. Treat it as a strongly implied light-air role rather than a confirmed roster entry.

**What is the Ebbtide Little Bird helicopter best at?**
Fast insertion and extraction. Its real value is moving squads and civilians between rooftops that ground vehicles cannot reach, plus light strafing against infantry clusters. It is not a frontline brawler.

**How many troops can it carry?**
Real-world MH-6 Little Birds seat up to six on external benches. If Ebbtide mirrors that, expect squad-sized lifts rather than platoon-scale air assault — enough for a fireteam plus a specialist.

**Will it be easy to shoot down?**
Light helicopters are fragile by design. Real Little Birds rely on speed, surprise, and terrain masking rather than armor, and an RTS adaptation will almost certainly reflect that with low health and high mobility. Fly it like a scalpel, not a hammer.
