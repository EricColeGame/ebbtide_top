## AH-6M Little Bird: Speed as a Weapon

The Little Bird is the most mobile confirmed piece on the board. In the showcase footage it departs from a flight deck and moves directly into a support role, which tells you a lot about how it is meant to be used: it is a reaction tool, not a frontline brawler.

Treat it that way and it earns its slot.

- **Plug leaks, don't hold lines.** Send it where a breach is forming, not where the fight is already stable.
- **Rotate early.** Light helicopters cannot trade blows with a swarm that touches them. Strike, break contact, rearm.
- **Keep it above the problem.** Anything that can reach into the air will punish a pilot who hovers over a horde.
- **Pair it with ground eyes.** Air support is most efficient when it hits what your armor has already pinned.

A common mistake in zombie RTS games is treating the fastest unit as the most important one. In practice, the Little Bird is a scalpel. If you use it as a hammer, you will be rebuilding it constantly.

## M2 Abrams: The Anchor of Your Line

If the Little Bird is the scalpel, the Abrams is the wall. The most striking community clip so far shows M2 Abrams tanks meeting a genuinely enormous zombie horde, and the framing makes the design intent obvious: armor exists to stand in the open and refuse to move.

That changes how you should think about **Ebbtide military units** built around heavy armor.

| Situation | Good Armor Answer | Bad Armor Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Wide open approach lane | Hull-down overwatch with infantry screening | Charging alone into a surround |
| Chokepoint or street | Two tanks staggered, one firing at a time | Stacking all armor in one spot |
| Horde flanking your base | Disengage and reposition | Stay put and hope the line holds |
| Mixed pressure across the map | Split armor, one anchor per lane | One tank sprinting between fronts |

Practical rules for tank commanders:

1. **Never let armor operate without a screen.** Zombies that reach the hull bypass the gun entirely.
2. **Position before contact.** A tank that starts moving during a breach is already losing.
3. **Rotate damaged armor behind the base.** Repair time is cheap; replacement time is not.
4. **Use tanks to buy time, not to win.** The horde is infinite; your hull count is not.

## Forward Operating Bases and Artillery: The Support Layer

The third pillar of any roster is the layer you build rather than drive. Footage shows players constructing forward operating bases and calling in artillery strikes while trying to hold Los Angeles, and that combination is what turns a static defense into a campaign.

A forward operating base does two jobs at once: it pushes your operational range outward, and it shortens the distance your units travel to rearm, repair, and return. Artillery does the opposite job — it lets you delete a problem before it ever reaches the line you built.

| Support Tool | Best Used For | Timing Window | Risk If Ignored |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forward operating base | Extending reach, sustaining pushes | Mid-match, once your first line stabilizes | Units spend the match walking instead of fighting |
| Artillery | Breaking up horde clusters | As density peaks, before contact | Your line absorbs the full wave |
| Defensive fallback position | Covering a retreat | Before you need it | A retreat becomes a rout |

Community reports suggest the pace of Ebbtide leans toward pressure that builds continuously rather than in discrete waves. If that holds, the practical advice is simple: build the base before you need it, and hold the artillery call for the moment the horde compresses into a kill zone.

## Force Composition by Phase

Knowing the units is not the same as knowing what to build. Here is a workable framework for splitting your **Ebbtide military units** across a match.

| Phase | Priority | Ideal Split | What You Are Buying |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | Establish, don't overextend | Ground forces + one fast responder | Information and map control |
| Early-mid | Survive the first real pressure | 1–2 tanks, base construction started | A line that holds without supervision |
| Mid | Extend and shape the fight | Armor anchors + forward base + artillery | Reach, sustain, and burst damage |
| Late | Absorb and counterpunch | Rotating armor, air support on call, artillery ready | The ability to survive a bad minute |

The trap in almost every survival RTS is over-investing in one leg of that triangle. All armor and no air means flanking pressure kills you. All air and no armor means you have nothing to hold ground with. All base and no mobile force means your infrastructure becomes the battlefield.

## FAQ

**Which Ebbtide military units have actually been shown so far?**

**Do forward operating bases really matter in Ebbtide?**

**Are Ebbtide military units realistic or arcade-style?**
The presentation leans modern-military: real-world vehicle names, flight-deck launches, artillery support, and base infrastructure. Exact simulation depth is not confirmed, so expect a spectrum between authentic hardware and approachable RTS pacing.
