## Bowrider vs. Cuddy: Choosing the Right Ebbtide Layout

Most of the lineup is open-bow seating, which tells you who Ebbtide built these boats for: families who want maximum cockpit space for the money. The 2100 Sport Cuddy is the notable exception, trading some open seating for an enclosed cabin. Neither layout is better — they simply solve different problems.

| Layout | What It Gives You | Best For | Trade-Offs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bowrider | Open bow seating, easy boarding, social cockpit | Day trips, tubing, swimming, first-time owners | Wet ride in chop, little weather protection |
| Cuddy cabin | Enclosed storage and shelter, lockable gear space | Overnighting, colder climates, gear-heavy trips | Less usable cockpit per foot, more areas to inspect |

A simple way to decide: count the people who will actually be aboard on a typical Saturday, not the maximum your imagination allows. Four to six people on a lake points hard toward a bowrider. Two adults who want a dry place for bags and a nap out of the wind will get more from a cuddy.

Storage matters too. A 19- to 21-foot bowrider fits a standard garage in many cases; a 25-footer usually does not. Measure your space before you fall in love with a listing — this is the most common regret buyers mention, and it belongs near the top of any Ebbtide vehicle guide.

## How to Inspect a Used Ebbtide Before You Buy

Bring a flashlight, a moisture meter if you own one, and a notebook. Two hours of methodical checking beats a five-minute walkaround every time.

| Check | What to Look For | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Hull and gelcoat | Spider cracks, impact repairs, keel scrapes | Reveals grounding or collision history |
| Deck and floor | Soft spots, flex underfoot, damp carpet edges | Often signals stringer or core moisture |
| Transom | Dull tap test, flex at the motor mounts | Structural repair is the costliest surprise |
| Engine | Compression numbers, hours, service records | Largest single line item in any budget |
| Electrical | Corroded terminals, working pumps, lights, bilge | Cheap to fix, annoying to discover late |
| Trailer | Tire age, bearings, lights, rated capacity | Towing safety and hidden replacement cost |
| Paperwork | Title, registration, hull ID | Confirms ownership and model year |
| Sea trial | Holeshot, planing, top RPM, steering feel | Verifies everything you inspected on land |

Run the engine on a hose before the water test, then insist on a real sea trial with the load you plan to carry. Check that the boat planes without excessive bow rise and that the tachometer reaches the manufacturer's recommended wide-open-throttle range with the propeller fitted.

For safety gear and legal requirements, review the [U.S. Coast Guard boating safety resources](https://www.uscgboating.org/) before your first outing. A voluntary vessel safety check is free in most areas and catches issues a casual inspection misses.

Community reports from owner forums are useful for spotting recurring complaints about a specific year or power package, but treat them as directional. One loud post is not a trend — look for patterns across multiple owners.

## Power, Capacity, and Tow Vehicle Match

A boat is a vehicle with its own registration, towing, and insurance rules, so treat the capacity plate as the most important sticker on board. It lists maximum persons, maximum weight, and maximum horsepower for that specific hull.

| Spec to Verify | Where to Find It | Why It Changes Your Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Max persons and weight | Capacity plate | Overloading hurts handling and insurance claims |
| Max horsepower | Capacity plate | Underpowered boats struggle to plane when loaded |
| Propeller pitch | Stamped on the prop hub | Wrong pitch means poor RPM and fuel economy |
| Fuel tank size | Deck fill, owner's manual | Range planning for longer lake days |
| Boat plus trailer weight | Trailer VIN plate, scale ticket | Must sit inside your tow vehicle's rating |

Never rely on a seller's memory for horsepower ratings or towing weights. Weigh the loaded trailer at a public scale if the numbers are close to your vehicle's limit, and remember that fuel, gear, and passengers all count against the rating.

This is the part of an Ebbtide vehicle guide that buyers skip and later regret: a perfectly good 21-foot bowrider is useless if your vehicle cannot pull it comfortably or your garage cannot swallow it.

## Budgeting for the First Year

Purchase price is only the entry ticket. Plan for the costs that arrive in the first twelve months, because they land fast and predictably.

| Cost Category | What to Plan For | Money-Saving Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline service | Impeller, fluids, plugs, fuel filters, belts | Bundle into one shop visit |
| Safety gear | Life jackets, throw cushion, extinguisher, flares | Buy to the capacity plate, not to guesswork |
| Trailer refresh | Tires, bearings, lights, spare | Tires age out even with low mileage |
| Storage | Marina slip, dry stack, or covered driveway | Compare seasonal versus monthly rates |
| Insurance | Hull and liability coverage | Ask about discounts for safety coursework |
| Annual upkeep | Winterization, bottom cleaning, misc. repairs | Set aside a monthly reserve |

Negotiate on condition, not on emotion. A boat with soft floors and cloudy service history is not a bargain at any price, and a well-documented one with higher hours is often the smarter buy.

Use the inspection table above as your negotiating script. Every item you can document becomes a line item you can discuss, and sellers respect a buyer who has clearly done the homework. That is the practical payoff of a good Ebbtide vehicle guide — leverage built on facts rather than vibes.

## FAQ

**What is an Ebbtide vehicle guide?**

It is a buyer-focused overview of Ebbtide's boat lineup — the models, lengths, layouts, and inspection points that matter when you are shopping the used market. **How many Ebbtide models are there?**

**What size Ebbtide is best for a first-time buyer?**

Most first-time owners are happiest in the 19- to 21-foot range. Those boats tow behind mid-size vehicles, fit many garages, and cost less to store and service. Step up only if your typical crew or your water demands the extra cockpit space.

**Are Ebbtide boats suitable for saltwater use?**

They can be, with discipline. Freshwater flushing after every trip, careful attention to trailer brakes and electrical connections, and prompt repairs to any nicks in the gelcoat make the biggest difference. Inspect a saltwater-kept boat more aggressively than a freshwater one, especially around the transom and engine mounting hardware.
