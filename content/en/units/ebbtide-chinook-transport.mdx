## Why Air Mobility Decides Zombie Sieges

Ground logistics break down the moment a horde cuts a street. That is the entire argument for the Ebbtide Chinook transport: it ignores the road network and creates its own supply lines through the air.

### Rooftop defense and vertical retreat

Rooftops are the strongest defensive terrain in a city survival game, and they are also the easiest place to get stranded. A transport lets you rotate exhausted defenders off a roof and drop fresh squads in without ever opening a ground corridor. Community reports from early footage emphasize rooftop defense as a core loop, which makes vertical rotation a genuine tactical priority rather than a novelty.

### Civilian evacuation under pressure

Evacuation objectives force you to split attention: hold a perimeter while moving non-combatants. Air lift compresses that problem. Load, climb, clear — then return for the next group. Ground convoys take the same risk but at a fraction of the speed.

### Forward operating base logistics

Base building is one of Ebbtide's three pillars, and forward bases are only as good as their supply line. A transport that can shuttle materials and reinforcements into a contested district keeps your front line advancing while your opponent's stalls.

Quick checklist for when air lift is worth the risk:

- The destination is closer by air than by any intact road.
- You can suppress the landing zone for at least the duration of one load cycle.
- Losing the aircraft would not collapse your entire plan.
- The cargo is worth more than the airframe.

## How to Deploy the Ebbtide Chinook Transport

Good transport play is a routine, not a reflex. Run the same sequence every time and your loss rate will fall.

1. **Scout first.** Identify a landing zone with cover on at least two sides before the aircraft leaves the pad.
2. **Suppress second.** Artillery or direct fire on the approach lane clears the space the aircraft needs.
3. **Load fast.** Embark and disembark cycles are the most dangerous moment of any lift.
4. **Climb out of reach.** Gain altitude and break line of sight before turning toward home.
5. **Escort the return leg.** The trip back is when players relax — and when transports die.
6. **Rotate crews.** Never run the same corridor twice in a row.
7. **Bank the airframe.** Keep one transport in reserve so a single loss is not a strategic defeat.

| Phase | Action | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Recon | Mark two landing zones, not one | A backup pad prevents panic reroutes |
| Prep | Fire support on the primary approach | Shortens hover time |
| Insert | Drop troops outside the horde's path | Avoids unloading into contact |
| Extract | Load wounded last, engines running | Minimizes exposure |
| Return | Vary altitude and route | Denies predictable ambush points |

## Keeping the Ebbtide Chinook Transport Alive

Most transport losses are self-inflicted. Players hover too long, fly the same lane twice, or send an unescorted aircraft into a district they have not scouted. The table below maps the usual threats to practical counters.

| Threat | Warning sign | Counter |
|---|---|---|
| Horde convergence | Density rising near the pad | Pre-strike with artillery before landing |
| Ground fire from occupied buildings | Tracers from windows | Clear adjacent structures with infantry first |
| Ambush on the return leg | Repeated safe trips | Change route and altitude every run |
| Overloading | Greedy cargo manifest | Prioritize troops over salvage |
| No reserve airframe | One aircraft in the roster | Build the second before pushing the front |

Community reports from early footage suggest scale is Ebbtide's signature — massive hordes, armored pushes, and heavy artillery all at once. Scale cuts both ways. It makes a successful evacuation spectacular, and it makes a stranded transport unrecoverable.
