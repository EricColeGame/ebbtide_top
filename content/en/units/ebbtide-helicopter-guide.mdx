## Why Air Support Changes the Fight in Ebbtide

Ebbtide's pitch is scale: hordes that pressure rooftop defenses, forward operating bases that need holding, and artillery called in to blunt a push. In that environment, helicopters solve a problem ground units can't — they collapse distance. A squad pinned on the far side of a block is minutes away on foot and seconds away by air. A breach in your perimeter that would take a fireteam three minutes to reach can be plugged almost immediately.

That mobility comes with real tradeoffs. Aircraft are expensive, conspicuous, and — in most RTS designs — fragile when they sit still. Community reports from the footage suggest the game rewards players who treat helicopters as precision instruments rather than flying tanks. Use them to buy time, then get them out.

| Helicopter role | The problem it solves | The risk you accept |
|---|---|---|
| Recon and overwatch | Finding horde movement before it reaches your line | Loitering too long over open streets |
| Close air support | Breaking up clusters pressing a barricade | Splash damage near your own infantry |
| Airlift and evacuation | Pulling survivors off isolated rooftops | Landing in a contested zone |
| Rapid reinforcement | Sealing a breach on the opposite flank | Arriving piecemeal instead of massed |

## Core Helicopter Roles and How to Fly Them

### Recon First, Shooting Second

The most underrated use of air power in any survival RTS is information. Send a helicopter up early to map the approach lanes, identify where the horde is massing, and confirm whether that side street is a shortcut or a death trap. A single recon pass can save you an entire infantry squad. In Ebbtide, where the threat is large-scale and directional, that intel advantage compounds across the whole match.

### Close Air Support That Doesn't Kill Your Own Troops

Air-to-ground fire is at its best when it hits the *front edge* of a horde, not the middle of your defensive line. Set your infantry back from the barricade, let the horde bunch up against it, then run the attack perpendicular to your own troops rather than parallel to them. If the game gives you any control over attack runs, always choose the vector that puts empty street behind your target.

### Evacuation and Airlift Discipline

Evacuation missions are one of the most interesting promises in Ebbtide, and they're also where new players lose helicopters. The rule is simple: clear the landing zone before you commit, not after. Suppress or thin the LZ with artillery or ground fire, set down long enough to load, and lift. If the LZ is hot, abort — a lost helicopter costs more than a delayed evacuation.

| Phase | What to do | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Approach | Fly high, scout the LZ from altitude | Diving straight in on the first pass |
| Set-down | Confirm the zone is clear before descending | Hovering over an uncleared rooftop |
| Loading | Keep the aircraft oriented toward your exit route | Turning your back on the threat axis |
| Departure | Climb immediately, gain altitude fast | Skimming low through street canyons |

## Positioning and Threat Management

Altitude is your armor. Zombies can't shoot, but they can swarm, and a helicopter forced to hover low over a packed street is one bad engine event away from becoming wreckage. Treat altitude as a resource you spend only when a mission demands it — landing, loading, or delivering a precise strike.

Noise is the other factor worth planning around. In survival games, aircraft frequently pull attention. If Ebbtide's hordes respond to sound, flying a helicopter over a quiet sector is a great way to make it loud, and that's useful when you're trying to pull pressure off a besieged base — and disastrous when you're trying to sneak an evacuation convoy through.

| Threat | Practical counter |
|---|---|
| Horde massing under your flight path | Route around, don't overfly |
| Anti-air or ranged special enemies | Scout first, strike from max range |
| Friendly fire onto your own line | Attack perpendicular to your infantry |
| Fuel, ammo, or cooldown limits | Rotate aircraft rather than overusing one |
| Losing air cover at the worst moment | Keep one airframe in reserve |

## Pairing Helicopters With Ground Forces

Air power in a vacuum is a waste. The Ebbtide helicopter guide that matters most is the combined-arms one: helicopters create openings, ground units exploit them. Armor absorbs pressure that would shred infantry; infantry holds ground that a helicopter can't occupy; artillery softens the exact cluster your aircraft is about to hit.

| Ground asset | Helicopter partner | Why the pairing works |
|---|---|---|
| Infantry squads | Recon helicopter | Air spots the horde, infantry sets the ambush |
| Armored vehicles | Close air support | Armor pins the horde, air hits the flank |
| Forward operating base | Transport helicopter | Air keeps the base supplied and reinforced |
| Artillery battery | Overwatch helicopter | Air spots targets, artillery does the damage |
| Evacuation convoys | Airlift helicopter | Air clears the route and lifts the stranded |

## Five Mistakes That Get Helicopters Destroyed

- **Committing air first.** Send ground units to make contact, then use helicopters to exploit what the contact reveals.
- **Hovering to admire the damage.** Fire, confirm the effect, and move. Stationary aircraft are targets.
- **Ignoring the reserve.** If your only helicopter is in the air, you have no air power — you have a gamble.
- **Using air against cheap targets.** Don't spend a helicopter on a handful of stragglers a rifle squad could handle.
- **Forgetting the objective.** Helicopters exist to enable evacuations, defenses, and pushes — not to farm kills.
