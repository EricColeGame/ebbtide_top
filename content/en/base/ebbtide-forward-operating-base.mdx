## Why the Ebbtide Forward Operating Base Decides the Campaign

Los Angeles is falling, and the one thing standing between an organized defense and a total rout is the Ebbtide forward operating base you plant in the ruins. In Ebbtide — a modern-military zombie survival RTS where you command infantry, armor, and artillery against relentless hordes — an Ebbtide forward operating base is the difference between a fighting force and a wandering column waiting to be surrounded. Get the base right, and everything downstream gets easier: resupply, fire support, air operations, and the ability to absorb a bad wave without losing the map.

That is the core lesson behind every community showcase of the game. Clips circulating from creator channels show Abrams tanks grinding through massive horde concentrations and AH-6M Little Bird helicopters lifting off from a flight deck — but those moments only happen when the ground beneath them is prepared. This guide breaks down how to plan, build, and hold an Ebbtide forward operating base so your armored fist has somewhere to come home to.

## What an Ebbtide Forward Operating Base Actually Does for You

Think of the base as the anchor of your entire command structure rather than a decorative cluster of buildings. It is where damaged units cycle out of contact, where artillery missions get coordinated, and where your aviation element stages before a sortie. A base that is sited well and layered correctly lets you trade space for time without collapsing.

| Base function | Why it matters on the map | When to prioritize |
|---|---|---|
| Resupply and repair | Keeps armor and infantry in the fight instead of limping home | Before your first major counterattack |
| Fire support coordination | Turns artillery from a panic button into a planned shield | As soon as horde waves begin escalating |
| Aviation staging | Lets rotary assets like the Little Bird operate from a forward deck | Once you have airframes worth protecting |
| Reinforcement staging | Cuts travel time for new units entering the line | Mid-game, when your front widens |
| Fallback anchor | Gives retreating units a rally point that holds | Any time your perimeter is longer than your garrison |

Community reports consistently describe Ebbtide as a game where scale is the selling point — enormous horde counts pressing against a modern military line. That scale is exactly why the base matters. When you are outnumbered by orders of magnitude, you do not win by spreading thin; you win by concentrating force around a structure that keeps generating it.

## Choosing a Site for Your Ebbtide Forward Operating Base

Site selection is the single decision you cannot easily undo. A beautiful kill zone on flat open ground becomes a deathtrap the moment a wave flanks it. Player experience suggests favoring terrain that does part of the defending for you: chokepoints, structural cover, and short interior lines.

| Site type | Strengths | Weaknesses | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Freeway interchange | Long sightlines, fast reinforcement lanes | Multiple approach vectors, hard to seal | Strong if you can garrison all ramps |
| Parking structure | Elevated firing positions, hard cover | Limited vehicle access, fire risk | Excellent for infantry-heavy holds |
| Stadium or arena lot | Wide staging area, clear fields of fire | Open ground on all sides | Good for armor, weak early |
| Port or warehouse district | Dense cover, narrow streets | Slow vehicle movement, easy to get cut off | Best for grinding, attritional play |
| Residential grid | Cheap cover everywhere | Poor sightlines, slow response | Defensive only, not a launch point |

Three rules that hold up across almost every layout:

- **Sit between the horde and what you cannot lose.** If the objective is downtown, your Ebbtide forward operating base belongs on the approach, not behind it.
- **Keep one open lane.** A fully enclosed base is a coffin. You need at least one route for armored units to sortie and for casualties to fall back.
- **Never build in a bowl.** Low ground hides your own units from your camera and your guns from the enemy's approach.

## Build Order: What Goes Up First Inside the Base

New commanders tend to overbuild walls and underbuild the things that keep walls standing. A better sequence is to secure the minimum viable perimeter, then invest in sustainment, then in firepower. Community footage of the game's showcases suggests that the firepower is spectacular — but it only arrives on schedule if the logistics chain behind it exists.

| Phase | Priority build | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Compact perimeter and one defended gate | You cannot lose what you can still seal |
| Early | Resupply and repair capacity | Damaged armor is dead armor |
| Early-mid | Artillery coordination point | Turns approaching waves into a manageable problem |
| Mid | Flight deck and aviation support | Extends reach far beyond your walls |
| Mid-late | Reinforced inner line | A second chance when the outer line fails |
| Late | Redundant fallback positions | Absorbs a breach without losing the campaign |

Two practical tips worth adopting immediately:

1. **Build the inner line before you need it.** Every experienced RTS player has watched an outer wall fall and realized there was nothing behind it.
2. **Keep the flight deck away from the main avenue of approach.** Rotary assets are fragile on the ground; the AH-6M Little Bird in Ebbtide's showcase launches from a deck, and a deck near the front door is a deck that gets overrun.

## Defending an Ebbtide Forward Operating Base Against Horde Pressure

Defense in Ebbtide is not a wall check — it is a rhythm. Waves build, break, and rebuild. Your job is to make each break longer than the last. The most reliable structure is a four-layer defense: an outer screen that slows, a kill zone that kills, an inner perimeter that holds, and a mobile reserve that plugs whatever fails.

| Pressure type | What it looks like | Recommended counter |
|---|---|---|
| Frontal mass | A dense push straight down your main approach | Massed artillery plus interlocking fire from the gate |
| Flanking probe | Smaller groups testing side walls and alleys | Mobile reserve and pre-registered fire missions |
| Sustained attrition | Constant low-level contact that grinds your garrison | Rotation through the base to repair and resupply |
| Escalation spike | Sudden surge after a quiet stretch | Hold artillery and armor in reserve for exactly this |

Armor should almost never be your static wall. The M2 Abrams footage that circulates in community clips shows tanks doing what tanks do best: punching a hole in a concentration and then pulling back under cover. Parked tanks become scenery; moving tanks stay alive.

Infantry, meanwhile, should be positioned so that every approach is covered by at least two angles. A single firing line can be overwhelmed by sheer weight of numbers. Two crossing lines create a kill zone where the horde's numerical advantage stops mattering.

## Air and Armor: Getting More From Your Ebbtide Forward Operating Base

The most impressive Ebbtide forward operating base is one that projects power, not one that simply survives. That means using the base as a launchpad rather than a bunker.

| Asset | Best use | Main risk | Support it needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main battle armor | Breaking up dense horde clusters | Isolation and flanking | Infantry escort and repair access |
| Rotary aviation | Fast response to breaches and flanking threats | Fragility while grounded | A protected deck and fuel/rearm cycle |
| Artillery | Shaping the battlefield before contact | Wasted on scattered targets | Forward observation and a coordinated base |
| Garrison infantry | Holding the line and sealing gaps | Attrition over long waves | Rotation and resupply |

A useful habit is to designate one "ready" asset at all times — a flight of rotary support or a tank platoon held at full strength and untouched. When the perimeter buckles, you want something to send that is not already exhausted. Community reports from long-session players echo the same point: the commanders who survive the late waves are the ones who never spent their entire reserve on the first crisis.

## Five Mistakes That Kill an Ebbtide Forward Operating Base

| Mistake | What it costs you | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Overextending the perimeter | Too much wall, too few defenders | Shrink the line until every meter is covered |
| Ignoring the inner line | One breach becomes a total loss | Build the fallback before the outer wall |
| Static armor | Tanks die in place | Keep armor as a reaction force |
| No reserve | Nothing left when the wave spikes | Hold 20–30% of your force uncommitted |
| Building in the open | Horde surrounds and isolates you | Anchor on terrain and structures |

Every one of these is recoverable early and fatal late. The pattern is consistent: commanders who treat the base as a fortress lose it, and commanders who treat it as a logistics engine with teeth keep it.

## FAQ

**What is the best location for an Ebbtide forward operating base?**
Look for a site with restricted approach routes, hard cover, and at least one fast lane for reinforcement. Freeway interchanges and parking structures tend to outperform open lots because they force the horde into predictable paths.

**How many defenders does an Ebbtide forward operating base need?**
Enough to cover every approach with overlapping fire, plus an uncommitted reserve. If you cannot cover a wall segment, shorten the perimeter rather than stretching your garrison across it.

**Should I build armor or aviation first?**
Armor first in most situations. Ground units are more forgiving, easier to repair, and less dependent on a protected flight deck — which is exactly what rotary assets like the Little Bird need to operate safely.

**Why does my Ebbtide forward operating base keep falling in later waves?**
Usually because the base was built to stop the first wave rather than the tenth. Add an inner line, keep a reserve, and invest in sustainment so your units can rotate out, repair, and return before the next surge arrives.
