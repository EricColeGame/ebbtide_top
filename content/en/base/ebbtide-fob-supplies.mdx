## Choosing a Site: Where a Forward Operating Base Earns Its Name

Placement is the cheapest decision you will ever make and the most expensive one to undo. Moving a stocked base costs you everything you spent building it, so spend ten extra minutes on the map before the first wall goes down.

| Criterion | Why it matters | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| Chokepoint access | Fewer approaches means fewer defenders needed | Open ground on three sides |
| Road and rail links | Faster, cheaper resupply runs | A single route that can be cut |
| Vertical options | Rooftops give sightlines and a fallback position | Flat ground with no elevation |
| Distance from horde lanes | Buys reaction time before contact | Sitting directly on a migration path |
| Fallback route | Lets you trade space for survival | A base with exactly one exit |
| Resource proximity | Shortens the whole supply chain | A long, exposed haul on every trip |

A few practical filters that work in almost any base-building game:

- **Draw the route before you draw the wall.** If you cannot describe how a truck gets in and out under fire, the site is wrong.
- **Prefer two mediocre approaches over one perfect one.** Redundancy beats efficiency when the enemy is a crowd.
- **Check the skyline.** Rooftop space is free real estate for overwatch, staging, and evacuation.
- **Ask what happens on turn one of a siege.** If the answer is "we lose the road," pick somewhere else.

## Building a Supply Loop That Doesn't Collapse

A stockpile is a snapshot; throughput is a trend. New commanders obsess over the number in the warehouse, while experienced ones watch how fast that number refills. Ebbtide FOB supplies only matter if they arrive where the fighting is, when the fighting is happening.

| Phase | Goal | Practical action |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Establish | Reach a minimum viable stock | Cap early stockpiling at roughly what one wave consumes |
| 2. Stockpile | Build a real buffer | Aim for a multi-wave reserve before expanding the footprint |
| 3. Distribute | Move supplies toward contact | Pre-position caches near likely defensive lines |
| 4. Replace | Stop losses from compounding | Rebuild during lulls, never during an active siege |

Two habits do most of the work here. First, **pre-position instead of centralizing.** A cache near the north wall is worth three caches in the courtyard, because the last hundred meters of any supply run is where you lose the truck. Second, **rebuild in the quiet.** Every minute of calm you spend restocking is a minute you won't spend panicking later.

## Defending the Base You Just Stocked

Defense exists to protect the supply chain, not the other way around. Every wall you build should answer a specific question: what does this stop, and what does it buy me? Layered defense is the standard answer because it converts a single catastrophic breach into a series of manageable problems.

| Layer | Job | Typical assets |
|---|---|---|
| Outer screen | Slow the horde and buy time | Wire, obstacles, forward outposts |
| Main line | Create the kill zone | Infantry squads, emplacements |
| Inner ring | Protect the stockpile itself | Fallback barricades, reserves |
| Vertical | Overwatch and evacuation | Rooftop positions, air support |
| Reaction force | Plug breaches fast | A mobile squad or armor kept uncommitted |

The reaction force is the layer most players skip. Keep one squad or vehicle out of the line. It feels wasteful right up until a wall fails, at which point it is the only thing standing between a breach and a rout.

## Five Mistakes That Starve a Base

Community reports and general survival-strategy experience converge on the same failure patterns. None of them are about being outplayed — they're about being unprepared.

| Mistake | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Stockpiling without throughput | Full warehouse, empty front line | Pre-position caches near contact points |
| Overbuilding early | Beautiful base, no ammunition | Build the minimum viable base first |
| Ignoring the vertical | No rooftop coverage at all | Assign dedicated overwatch |
| One-route logistics | Base starves the moment the road is cut | Engineer a second approach |
| No reserve | The first breach ends the run | Keep one unit permanently uncommitted |

## FAQ

**What are Ebbtide FOB supplies?**
They're the resources, materiel, and personnel that keep a forward operating base functioning — ammunition, fuel, construction materials, medical support, and the logistics routes that move them. In practice, Ebbtide FOB supplies are the difference between a base that projects force across the map and one that simply waits to be surrounded.

**How much should I stockpile at a forward operating base?**
A useful rule of thumb is a multi-wave buffer before you expand your footprint. If your reserve cannot absorb two or three consecutive assaults without a resupply run, you're operating on luck rather than logistics.

**Where should I place my first FOB?**
Prioritize a chokepoint with two viable supply routes, some vertical space for overwatch, and a fallback path. Distance from major horde lanes matters more than proximity to resources early on, because time to react is worth more than a shorter haul.
