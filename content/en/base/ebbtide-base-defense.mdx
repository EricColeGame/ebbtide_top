## Why Ebbtide Base Defense Decides Your Run

The moment a zombie horde collapses your outer wall in Ebbtide, you stop thinking about base building as a cozy side activity and start treating Ebbtide base defense as the spine of your entire run. Ebbtide blends squad-based combat, base construction, and large-scale zombie survival into one ambitious package, which means every wall you place is also a tactical decision about where your soldiers will live or die. Get Ebbtide base defense right and the horde becomes a resource problem you can manage; get it wrong and you spend the rest of the campaign rebuilding instead of pushing back.

Ebbtide is still in development, so specific numbers, unit rosters, and balance values shown in community footage may change before launch. What will not change is the underlying logic of defending a fixed position against a numerically superior enemy. That logic is what this guide covers.

## What Ebbtide Base Defense Actually Demands

According to community reports and early gameplay showcases, Ebbtide puts you in command of the fight for Los Angeles. You can play as a single survivor on the ground or step back into a commander role, directing squads, armored vehicles, and helicopters while enormous zombie hordes press against your positions. ### The Three Jobs Every Defense Must Do

| Job | What it accomplishes | How it usually fails |
|---|---|---|
| Delay | Slows the horde before it reaches your wall | Obstacles placed too close together, or none at all |
| Channel | Funnels attackers into predictable lanes | Open ground with no choke points |
| Destroy | Kills what reaches the kill zone | Firepower with no ammunition or repair support |

If your Ebbtide base defense only does one of these three, you have a speed bump, not a fortress.

## The Four Layers of a Reliable Ebbtide Base Defense

Depth beats thickness. A single enormous wall looks impressive until it breaches, at which point everything behind it is undefended. Layered defenses give you time to react, and time is the only currency that matters when a horde is sprinting at your gate.

| Layer | Primary purpose | Typical assets | Warning sign it's too thin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Outer screen | Slow and reveal the horde | Wire, mines, scattered barricades, scout patrols | Enemies arrive at your wall at full speed |
| 2. Wall line | Stop and channel attackers | Reinforced walls, gates, choke points | One breach turns into three |
| 3. Kill zone | Destroy whatever gets through | Entrenched infantry, vehicles, heavy weapons | The wall absorbs damage but nothing dies |
| 4. Core and FOB | Sustain the fight | Command post, medics, ammo, repair bay, evac route | You win the battle and still lose the base |

### Why Depth Beats Thickness

A thick wall has one failure point: itself. A layered Ebbtide base defense has five or six places where an attack can be absorbed, slowed, and answered. When a breach opens in a layered setup, the horde pours into a kill zone that was designed for exactly that moment. When a breach opens in a single-wall setup, the horde pours into your supply depot.

Practical rule: build the second line before you upgrade the first. A basic wall with a working fallback position outperforms a maxed-out wall with nothing behind it.

## Build Order: What to Construct First

Community footage suggests forward operating bases can be established and pushed forward as you expand, so your build order should be repeatable rather than a one-time plan. Use the same sequence every time you plant a new base.

| Phase | Priority builds | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Command post, basic power and logistics, one wall segment facing the known approach | Establishes the anchor point and gives you something to defend |
| Early | Ammunition storage, repair capability, second firing line, rooftop access | Turns a structure into a fighting position |
| Mid | Vehicle bay, artillery support, marked evacuation route | Adds reach and a way out |
| Late | Redundant storage, a second forward base, a mobile reserve squad | Survives losing a layer instead of losing the run |

Two habits separate strong builders from frustrated ones. First, never build a wall you cannot walk behind — if defenders cannot reposition along the inside of the line, the wall is a cage. Second, always leave at least one gate you control rather than one you need to destroy.

## Rooftops, Kill Zones, and Artillery Timing

Rooftop defenses are one of the most valuable tools in Ebbtide base defense because elevation solves two problems at once. It reduces the number of angles an enemy can reach you from, and it gives your survivors a place to hold while civilians evacuate below. Treat rooftops as fighting positions, not scenery.

Kill zones work on a simple principle: no attacker should be able to run in a straight line toward something you care about. Angle your walls, stagger your barricades, and overlap fields of fire so that anything entering the gap is being shot from at least two directions.

Artillery is the great multiplier and the great liability. Pre-plan your impact zones before the horde arrives, keep them outside your own wall line, and target clusters in the approach lanes rather than individual enemies that have already breached. Firing danger-close to plug a hole is how defenders get deleted by their own support.

| Threat | Best response | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Massed slow horde | Artillery on the approach lane, then heavy weapons in the kill zone | Committing vehicles to the wall itself |
| Fast flankers | Mobile reserve squad plus rooftop overwatch | Relying only on static walls |
| Heavily armored targets | Direct-fire vehicles and concentrated damage | Spreading fire across many targets |
| Breach inside the wire | Reserve squad plus fallback line | Pulling every defender off the wall |
| Civilian evacuation | Dedicated escort squad and a cleared route | Using your only reserve as the escort |

## Squad Roles in a Working Ebbtide Base Defense

You cannot personally cover every gap, so your squads need defined jobs before the shooting starts. Assign roles in advance and keep them stable; a squad that improvises every wave will eventually improvise into a wall.

| Role | Core responsibility | Deployment note |
|---|---|---|
| Line infantry | Hold the wall and plug breaches | Static, rotated for rest and resupply |
| Engineers and support | Repair structures, rebuild, emplace defenses | Positioned one layer back, not on the wall |
| Vehicle crews | Mobile firepower and counterattacks | Held as a reaction force, not a wall ornament |
| Air support | Reconnaissance and rapid response | Best used to spot hordes before they arrive |
| Command element | Coordination and buffs | Protected, never exposed to direct fire |

Vehicles are tempting to park on the front line because they look indestructible. In practice, a tank committed to a fixed position becomes a target with no escape route. Keep armor behind the wall line and use it to erase breaches, not to decorate them.

## Common Ebbtide Base Defense Mistakes

- **Building wide instead of deep.** A long thin perimeter requires more defenders than you will ever have.
- **No ammunition or repair economy.** Firepower without resupply is a two-minute advantage.
- **Ignoring the evacuation route.** Civilian evacuations appear in showcases for a reason; if the route is blocked, the escort mission becomes a rescue mission.
- **Over-investing in one position.** Spread critical infrastructure so a single breach cannot end the run.
- **Never leaving the commander view.** You need to see the whole base, not just the fight in front of your rifle.

| Mistake | Symptom you'll notice | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Flat, open perimeter | Hordes hit the wall from three sides at once | Add angled walls and choke points |
| No reserve | Every breach becomes a crisis | Keep one squad uncommitted |
| Late artillery | Support fires after the wall is already down | Pre-plan impact zones during the build phase |
| Neglected rooftops | Civilians die during evacuations | Convert roofs into firing positions early |

## Practical Tips for Holding the Line

- Walk your perimeter before every wave and ask one question at each point: where do I fall back to from here?
- Build the fallback line before you upgrade the front line.
- Keep one squad and one vehicle uncommitted at all times. A reserve that has never fired is still doing its job.
- Mark your artillery zones physically so you never have to think about them under pressure.
- Rotate defenders. Fatigue and empty magazines lose more bases than enemy strength does.

## FAQ

**Is Ebbtide base defense the same thing as base building?**
No. Base building is the construction layer — walls, power, production, storage. Ebbtide base defense is the tactical layer on top of it: how those structures are arranged, what covers them, where your squads stand, and how you respond when something breaks. A well-built base with a bad defense plan falls faster than a modest base with a good one.

**Where should I place my first forward operating base?**
Pick a location with limited approach lanes and at least one elevated position you can use for overwatch. Avoid open ground in the middle of a district, even if the resources look better there — you will spend more defending it than you gain.

**How many layers should a good Ebbtide base defense have?**
Four is a reasonable target: an outer screen to slow attackers, a wall line to channel them, a kill zone to destroy them, and a core with support infrastructure. If you can only manage two, make it a wall and a fallback position.

**Does base defense matter if I prefer playing as a survivor instead of commanding?**
Yes, arguably more. When you are on the ground fighting, the base is running on whatever plan you left behind. Layered Ebbtide base defense is what keeps your position intact while you are personally busy somewhere else.
