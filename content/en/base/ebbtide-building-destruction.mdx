Every wall you raise in Ebbtide can come down — and the game remembers exactly when and where it happened. Ebbtide building destruction is not a cosmetic particle effect that resets when the camera pans away; it is a permanent change to a battlefield that keeps simulating whether you are watching or not. That single design choice reshapes how you spend ammunition, where you place a forward operating base, and when it is smarter to abandon a district than to bleed a battalion defending rubble.

Ebbtide is a real-time zombie survival strategy game from developer and publisher ProjectAlive, currently listed as "Coming soon" on Steam. You command TF-1 "Ark," humanity's last carrier task force, starting in Los Angeles and carrying the campaign across the world. Because there are no separate missions and no hidden auto-resolve, every structure you lose stays lost on the operations map.

## What Ebbtide Building Destruction Means on the Operations Map

The core pitch is that the map *is* the battlefield. Scroll into any street on the operations map and it becomes the live battlefield with no loading screen; scroll back out and the fight keeps running. Nothing is abstracted away into a dice roll. According to the official Steam listing, every soldier lost, zombie killed, and building destroyed carries back to the map.

That makes Ebbtide building destruction a logistics problem as much as a combat problem. A collapsed wall is not just a hole in your perimeter — it is building material you have to haul in again, a firing position you no longer have, and possibly a road your convoys can no longer use.

| Event on the battlefield | What it costs you on the operations map |
|---|---|
| A wall section goes down | Replacement materials must be trucked or sling-loaded in |
| A forward base is overrun | A rearm and refuel point disappears from your network |
| A convoy is ambushed | Construction and ammunition deliveries stop arriving |
| A district slides to Lost | Staging ground, cover, and line of sight are gone |
| A soldier is killed by the horde | That unit is gone — and may come back as an enemy |

The practical takeaway: treat every structure as expendable but every *position* as precious. Ask not "can I hold this building?" but "what does losing it cost me in supply distance?"

## The Firepower Behind Ebbtide Building Destruction

The Steam page is blunt: ship magazines are finite, and heavy weapons bring buildings down. That one sentence is the entire economy of Ebbtide building destruction in miniature. Every structure you flatten costs something you cannot print on demand — a missile cell, a rocket pod, a sortie from the carrier deck.

The confirmed firepower list breaks into four families:

- **Ground artillery:** M777 howitzers and HIMARS rockets
- **Naval fires:** destroyer guns and Tomahawk cruise missiles
- **Air strikes:** carrier F/A-18s, plus F-15E, F-35B, and A-10 strikes
- **Persistent air support:** AC-130J gunship and MQ-9 drone orbits

| System | Where it comes from | Structural role | Main cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| M777 howitzers | Ground batteries | Sustained indirect fire into built-up blocks | Ammunition resupply by convoy |
| HIMARS rockets | Ground launchers | Fast, heavy response when a position must go now | Limited pods |
| Destroyer guns and Tomahawks | Carrier group escorts | Offshore fire support reaching into the city | Finite ship magazines |
| F/A-18, F-15E, F-35B, A-10 | Carrier and land air | Precision or sustained attack runs | Airframe availability and fuel |
| AC-130J and MQ-9 | Loitering aircraft | Overwatch and fire support over a district | Orbit time and fuel |

The design tension is obvious once you play: the same tools that clear a horde off your perimeter are the tools that erase the cover you wanted to keep. Before you call in a strike, decide whether you are removing a threat or removing a future firing position.

## Building a Base That Survives Ebbtide Building Destruction

Base building in Ebbtide is deliberate rather than instant. You choose where to dig in, then raise HESCO and concrete walls, add gates and scaffolding to defend the wall line, and place gun nests, CIWS, mortar pits, an ammo depot, a fuel point, and lighting. Supply is what keeps all of it running — soldiers, vehicles, and aircraft burn through ammunition and fuel, and they can only rearm and refuel at bases you hold.

Every wall also needs building materials hauled in by hooklift truck or helicopter sling. That means your defensive plan and your logistics plan are the same document.

| Structure | Job in the base | Build priority |
|---|---|---|
| HESCO and concrete walls | Primary barrier against the horde | Highest — everything else sits behind it |
| Gates | Controlled movement for friendlies | High — but every gate is a weak point |
| Scaffolding | Lets defenders fight from the wall line | High on threatened faces |
| Gun nests | Close-in perimeter defense | High |
| CIWS | Point defense against fast threats | Medium-high |
| Mortar pits | Indirect fire beyond the wall | Medium |
| Ammo depot | Rearm point for the garrison | Medium-high |
| Fuel point | Refuel for vehicles and aircraft | Medium |
| Lighting | Visibility during night pressure | Medium |

### Common mistakes that accelerate Ebbtide building destruction

- **Walling everything at once.** Materials arrive by truck and sling, so a sprawling perimeter starves itself.
- **Ignoring the road network.** The horde hunts convoys and cuts roads; a base you cannot resupply is already lost.
- **Building inside the fight.** Dig in where you can stockpile, not where the horde is already climbing.
- **Skipping lighting.** A dark wall line is a wall line you cannot defend.
- **Over-committing heavy weapons to base defense.** Ship magazines are finite; spend them on threats, not on scenery.

## How the Horde Breaks What You Build

Zombies in Ebbtide are not a wave that walks into your guns. They pile up to climb walls and rooftops, and they cling to helicopters hovering close to the ground. Soldiers killed by the horde rise again as zombies — meaning a failed defense can literally reinforce the attack against you.

| Horde behavior | Why it threatens your base | Practical counter |
|---|---|---|
| Piling up to climb walls and rooftops | Bypasses your perimeter vertically | Overlapping fields of fire; defenders on scaffolding |
| Clinging to low-hovering helicopters | Threatens your air logistics directly | Keep hover time short and escort lifts |
| Killing soldiers who then rise | Your own perimeter feeds the enemy | Contain casualties fast; do not leave bodies inside the wire |
| Hunting convoys and cutting roads | Starves construction and rearming | Escort routes and vary timing |

The rooftop climb is the detail that changes base design most. A wall that is perfect against ground attack is a staircase if the buildings beside it are still standing — and in Ebbtide, they usually are.

## District Pressure: When to Abandon a Position

Los Angeles is split into 15 districts that slide from Hold to Crisis to Lost. You can pause the operations map or run it at up to 4x speed, and you cannot save everyone — so the real skill is deciding who to pull out first.

You can order National Guard, LAPD, and LA County Sheriff's units to hold, delay, or withdraw, and call in Marines from Camp Pendleton. Truck and helicopter convoys move supplies, troops, or refugees. Units and survivors cut off behind the lines can be extracted before the horde reaches them. Note that only hordes within your forces' line of sight appear on the map, so scouting is not optional.

| District state | What it means | Sensible response |
|---|---|---|
| Hold | Under control and usable | Fortify, stockpile, use as a logistics hub |
| Crisis | Contested or slipping | Reinforce briefly, or delay and prepare evacuation |
| Lost | Overrun | Stop feeding units in; extract survivors and salvage what you can |

Once Los Angeles resolves, the people, troops, equipment, and supplies you bring to the Port of LA become your starting force aboard the Ark for the world campaign. You choose which regions to help, bring the carrier group's guns and aircraft within range, resupply at friendly ports, grow the fleet with allied warships, and hunt the Nexus's hidden command nodes.

## FAQ

**Can you rebuild destroyed buildings in Ebbtide?**
The Steam listing confirms that building destruction persists on the operations map, but ProjectAlive has not detailed a repair or reconstruction system. What is confirmed is that you can build and expand forward operating bases yourself, so plan on re-establishing positions with hauled-in materials rather than restoring rubble for free.

**What causes the most Ebbtide building destruction?**
Heavy weapons. Howitzers, HIMARS, destroyer guns, Tomahawk cruise missiles, and aircraft strikes are all confirmed to bring structures down. Because ship magazines are finite, every demolition is a trade against future fire support.

**Why does Ebbtide building destruction matter for base design?**
Because walls consume materials delivered by hooklift truck or helicopter sling, and because the horde hunts those convoys. A base that is too large to keep supplied will lose sections faster than you can rebuild them — compact, well-lit, well-covered positions survive longer.
