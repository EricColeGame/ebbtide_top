## What the Ebbtide Announcement Trailer Actually Shows

The reveal is structured as a tour of systems rather than a cinematic. Each segment introduces one mechanic, then hands off to the next. Here is how the beats break down.

| Trailer beat | What's on screen | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Map zoom | Camera dives from an operations map into a live street | No loading screens, no separate mission instances |
| District pressure | LA's 15 districts shifting between Hold, Crisis, and Lost | You cannot save everyone; triage is the core loop |
| Convoys | Truck and helicopter groups moving supplies, troops, refugees | Logistics is a target, not a background number |
| Horde behavior | Zombies stacking to reach rooftops, clinging to low helicopters | The enemy interacts with terrain and air units |
| Fire support | Howitzers, rockets, naval guns, airstrikes, gunship orbits | Heavy ordnance is finite and collapses buildings |
| Base construction | Walls, gates, scaffolding, gun nests, ammo and fuel points | You build the supply network you fight from |
| Campaign handoff | Port of LA forces loading onto the carrier Ark | The city is a prologue to a world map |

The most important line in the whole reveal is conceptual: the map *is* the battlefield. There are no separate missions and no hidden auto-resolve. Every soldier lost, zombie killed, and building destroyed carries back to the strategic layer.

### The map never stops running

In the Ebbtide announcement trailer, the camera pulls back out of a firefight and the fight keeps going. You can pause, or run the operations map at up to 4x speed, while districts slide from Hold to Crisis to Lost. That design choice means the trailer is showing a game with real time pressure rather than turn-based abstraction — and it means new players should expect to lose ground early while learning the pacing.

### Fog of war that respects line of sight

Only hordes inside your forces' line of sight appear on the map. This is a subtle detail that changes how you read the trailer: those clean district overlays are not omniscient. Reconnaissance and screening units matter as much as firepower.

## Reading the Firepower and Logistics Beats

The weapons montage is the part casual viewers remember, but the trade-offs are the actual story. Ship magazines are finite, and heavy weapons bring buildings down — which cuts both ways in a city you are trying to hold.

| Asset shown | Role | Trade-off implied |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M777 howitzers, HIMARS | Long-range suppression of hordes | Ammunition burn, collateral damage |
| Destroyer guns, Tomahawk missiles | Offshore fire support from the carrier group | Finite magazine depth |
| F/A-18, F-15E, F-35B, A-10 strikes | Fast response against massed targets | Coordination and availability |
| AC-130J gunship, MQ-9 drone orbits | Persistent overwatch | Vulnerable to being overrun |
| CIWS, gun nests, mortar pits | Base and wall defense | Requires supply to keep firing |

Base building follows the same logic. You choose where to dig in, raise HESCO and concrete walls, add gates and scaffolding, then place gun nests, CIWS, mortar pits, an ammo depot, a fuel point, and lighting. Every wall needs materials hauled in by hooklift truck or helicopter sling. Soldiers, vehicles, and aircraft only rearm and refuel at bases you hold — and the horde actively hunts convoys and cuts roads. Community reports from strategy players who have watched the Ebbtide announcement trailer repeatedly tend to fixate on this: the trailer sells a logistics game wearing a zombie costume.

## From Los Angeles to the World

The final act of the reveal reframes everything before it. The people, troops, equipment, and supplies you evacuate to the Port of LA become your starting force aboard the Ark for the world campaign. From there you choose which regions to help, bring the carrier group's guns and aircraft within range, resupply at friendly ports, rescue pilots and specialists you cannot train, grow the fleet with allied warships, and hunt the Nexus's hidden command nodes.

In practical terms, that means your Los Angeles decisions are a save-file seed. Pulling out the wrong specialists early could leave you without capabilities later — a detail worth noting while rewatching the Ebbtide announcement trailer's convoy footage.

## FAQ

**Is there an official Ebbtide announcement trailer?**
Yes. The reveal footage lives on the game's Steam page, which leads with a gameplay trailer of roughly 1 minute 36 seconds plus several shorter feature clips covering the map, hordes, firepower, bases, and the world campaign.

**When does Ebbtide release?**
No date has been announced. The store lists "coming soon," and final system requirements — including processor and graphics specs — will be confirmed before launch. Minimum storage is listed at 10 GB on Windows 10 64-bit with DirectX 11.

**Is Ebbtide multiplayer?**
No. It is listed as single-player with Family Sharing support, so the campaign is a solo command experience rather than a co-op one.

**Does the Ebbtide announcement trailer confirm console versions?**
Not currently. Only Windows and macOS are listed, and Steam Deck compatibility is marked unknown. Any console announcement would come later from ProjectAlive.

**What should new players take away from the reveal?**
That this is a logistics and triage game first. Firepower is limited, districts will fall, and the Ebbtide announcement trailer is honest about that: you cannot save everyone, so decide who you pull out first.
