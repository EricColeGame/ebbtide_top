## How Ebbtide Troop Transport Fits Into the RTS Loop

Ebbtide is a stylized 3D zombie survival RTS in development, and it plays out on two connected scales. On the operations map you direct forces across Los Angeles; once you commit, the camera zooms into the battlefield where you command troops directly or take control of a unit yourself. Ebbtide troop transport is the bridge between those two layers — it's how a decision made on a map becomes boots on a street.

That two-scale design is why lift matters more here than in a typical RTS. You aren't just spawning units at a building; you're routing aircraft across a city, choosing where they touch down, and deciding how much of your force arrives at once. Community reports consistently point to the same lesson: players who plan the insertion before the shooting starts lose far fewer squads than players who scramble aircraft mid-fight.

There's also a logistics chain underneath the combat. Forward operating bases have to be built and then supplied, and the developer's own update titles make the point bluntly — a FOB won't resupply itself. Ebbtide troop transport is the answer to that problem, moving people and cargo from ships and rear areas to the places where the line actually needs holding.

### Commander mode vs. direct control

Both approaches work, but they solve different problems. In commander mode you can manage multiple lifts at once — a transport formation inbound, a second wave spinning up, a resupply run queued behind them. Taking direct control is better for a single precision drop or for escorting a fragile lift through a hot corridor. The practical habit is to stay in commander mode until the first wave is committed, then jump in only when a specific aircraft needs babysitting.

## Formations, Spacing, and Rotor Wash

Recent developer updates have focused hard on helicopter group movement: different formations, adjustable spacing between aircraft, and larger transport formations crossing Los Angeles before releasing squads on the ground. That's a meaningful mechanical shift. It means Ebbtide troop transport is not a single click that teleports a squad — it's a small formation-management problem every time you fly.

Spacing is the setting that will decide most of your drops. Tight spacing gets your force concentrated quickly, which is exactly what you want when a FOB is about to be overrun. Wide spacing disperses arrival times and reduces the chance that one burst of fire catches three aircraft at once, but it also means your squads trickle in instead of arriving as a fist.

| Situation | Spacing approach | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Contested rooftop or street LZ | Wide spacing, staggered arrival | Slower to mass, far fewer losses to concentrated fire |
| Emergency reinforcement of a FOB | Tight formation | Fast massing, risky if the corridor is still hot |
| Crossing open city blocks | Dispersed formation | Harder to escort, but a single ambush can't catch everyone |
| Sling-loading cargo | Wide spacing with escort | Slower throughput, safer for the cargo run |

Rotor wash is the other detail worth watching. Developer footage has highlighted rotor wash effects alongside troop transport and rappelling, and while those effects are visual first, they're also a timing cue. A helicopter that's still settling is a helicopter that isn't dropping yet. Learning to read the sequence — approach, hover, wash, release — helps you time the ground push instead of guessing.

## Ship-to-Shore Logistics and Rappelling

The naval layer is where Ebbtide troop transport gets genuinely interesting. The destroyer showcase shows deck crew, a close-in weapon system, and helicopters landing on the stern flight deck, picking up squads, and taking off again. One aircraft also carries sling-loaded cargo underneath. Carrier operations go further, with pilots boarding fighters, launches, deck landings, taxiing, parking, and pilots climbing out afterward.

What that means for a player is a full rotation cycle rather than a one-way trip. Ship decks act as staging points: land, load, launch, deliver, return. Every extra second on deck is a second your squads aren't on the objective, so the players who internalize the cycle early will simply out-tempo everyone else.

| Delivery method | Speed | Exposure | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Land and disembark | Moderate | High — aircraft is stationary | Secured LZs, FOB reinforcement |
| Rappel from hover | Fast | Moderate — no touchdown needed | Rooftops and tight urban gaps |
| Sling load | Slow | Low for troops, high for cargo | Supplies and equipment without a full landing |
| Deck transfer | Slowest | Very low | Rotating squads between ship and shore |

Rappelling deserves its own note because it directly answers Ebbtide's level design. The developer's messaging has been consistent that rooftops aren't safe — the horde gets up there too. Rappelling still beats landing when the street is blocked or the roof is the only flat surface, but it puts troops in a space where they can be surrounded. Treat rappel insertion as a way to claim high ground fast, not as a way to avoid a fight.

## Practical Checklist: Better Ebbtide Troop Transport Runs

Theory only pays off if it changes what you do in the first two minutes. Use this checklist as a pre-flight routine.

| Step | Action | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pick the LZ before you launch | Aircraft that circle looking for a spot are aircraft that get shot at |
| 2 | Send the escort first | Armed helicopters clear a corridor; transports shouldn't be the first thing over the target |
| 3 | Set spacing to match the threat | Tight for emergencies, wide for contested airspace |
| 4 | Land the vehicle and infantry together | Chinook loads keep squads mobile on arrival |
| 5 | Queue the resupply before the first squad lands | A FOB without supplies is a building, not a base |
| 6 | Keep a return leg planned | Deck rotations need a clear lane home |

A few habits worth building on top of that list:

- **Never fly a full transport into an unscouted block.** Send something expendable or something armed first.
- **Stagger your drops across two or three LZs** so one bad landing doesn't cost you the entire wave.
- **Watch your spacing when the horde is thick on the ground.** Wide formations buy time, and time is the resource Ebbtide troop transport actually spends.
- **Use the operations map for planning, not reaction.** Committing aircraft from the map layer before the fight starts keeps the battlefield layer calm.
- **Practice rappelling early.** It's the skill that turns a blocked street into a non-issue.

Community reports also flag a common beginner mistake: treating transports as disposable. Losing one helicopter usually costs more than losing the squad inside it, because the airframe is what lets you try again. If a drop looks bad, wave off and re-approach — Ebbtide troop transport rewards patience far more than it rewards speed.

## FAQ

**What is Ebbtide troop transport?**
It's the collection of mechanics that move infantry and cargo around the battlefield — helicopter formations with adjustable spacing, landings, rappelling, sling-loaded cargo, and ship-deck staging. In practice, Ebbtide troop transport is the system that connects the Los Angeles operations map to the fights on the ground.

**Can helicopters carry vehicles in Ebbtide?**
Yes, at least in part. Developer footage shows the Chinook carrying both squads and Humvees, which makes combined vehicle-and-infantry drops possible. Other airframes appear focused on lighter squad movement and armed escort.

**Is rappelling better than landing?**
It depends on the LZ. Rappelling avoids the need for a touchdown, which is valuable on rooftops and in tight urban gaps, but it drops troops into an exposed position. Landing is safer when the ground is already secured; rappelling is better when it isn't and you need height immediately.
