## What Ebbtide Base Building Brings to the Zombie Survival RTS

Most zombie games ask you to pick a lane. You either micromanage a single survivor with a shotgun and three bullets, or you sit above the map like a chess grandmaster and never touch the ground. Ebbtide's pitch is that you get both, and the base layer is the bridge between them.

Community reports from early footage describe a loop where you can fight directly as a survivor, order infantry squads around, roll M2 Abrams tanks into a horde, and call in helicopter support — all while rooftop defenses and civilian evacuations are happening around you. Player experience so far suggests the base is what makes those tools sustainable. Without a place to rearm, regroup, and project power from, every vehicle and every squad is a one-way trip.

The comparison to other zombie RTS titles is inevitable. Players have already drawn lines between Ebbtide and games like Cepheus Protocol, which is a fair reference point for anyone who likes the idea of military hardware versus an overwhelming infected population. The distinction Ebbtide appears to be chasing is scale plus direct control: you are not just a commander cursor, and you are not just a lone survivor hiding in a stairwell.

| Layer | What you actually do | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Hands-on survival | Fight as a single survivor on the ground | Lets you plug gaps a squad AI might miss |
| Squad command | Direct infantry, armor, and helicopters | Turns a static defense into a mobile one |
| Ebbtide base building | Establish FOBs, rooftop defenses, emplacements | Provides sustain, staging, and fire support |
| Support calls | Artillery and air assets | Deletes density the horde creates |

## The Core Loop: Establish, Fortify, Expand

Every base-building system in a survival RTS lives or dies on its loop. If the loop is "build wall, watch wall break, rebuild wall," players quit. If the loop is "build, defend, learn, push outward," it holds attention for dozens of hours.

From what has been shown, Ebbtide base building appears to follow a three-beat rhythm: you establish a foothold, you fortify it enough to survive the next wave, and you expand toward objectives like civilian evacuation routes or better firing lines. The temptation is always to over-fortify one position. The horde does not care how pretty your perimeter is if it can simply flow around it.

| Phase | Primary goal | Where your resources should go | Classic mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| Establish | Claim a defensible foothold | Sightlines, access roads, fallback space | Building in the open for convenience |
| Fortify | Survive the first serious wave | Layered barriers, elevated firing positions | One thick wall with no depth |
| Expand | Push toward objectives and evacuations | Forward operating bases, mobile assets | Abandoning the original base entirely |
| Sustain | Keep squads and vehicles in the fight | Repair, resupply, rotation of units | Treating vehicles as disposable |

A useful rule of thumb: never spend two full waves' worth of effort on a position you would abandon the moment a tank needs to drive through it. Ebbtide base building rewards layouts that let heavy assets move, not layouts that look tidy on a screenshot.

## Site Selection and Layout Priorities

Location is the single highest-leverage decision in any base-building system, and it is the one most players rush. In a game where the enemy arrives in volume rather than precision, the terrain does most of your killing for you — walls just help.

Here is a practical weighting for site selection. It is not a stat sheet, because the developers have not published one, but it maps cleanly onto what the footage implies about how hordes approach and how vehicles maneuver.

| Criteria | Why it matters | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Choke points and natural funnels | Compresses the horde into kill zones | Very high |
| Elevated access (rooftops, upper floors) | Improves sightlines and protects shooters | Very high |
| Vehicle circulation | Tanks and helicopters need room to operate | High |
| Distance to evacuation routes | Shortens escort runs and civilian exposure | High |
| Multiple exit paths | Prevents total loss when a line collapses | Medium |
| Aesthetic symmetry | Looks great in a clip, kills you in a wave | Ignore |

Rooftop defenses deserve their own paragraph. Community footage leans heavily on verticality, and it is easy to see why: a survivor on a roof can shoot down into a crowd that cannot easily reach them, and a rooftop position is much harder to flank than a street-level sandbag line. The catch is that verticality is also a trap. If your only ammo, medkits, and reinforcements are up top, a single collapsed stairwell turns a strongpoint into a coffin. Build up, but keep a ground-level lifeline.

## Layered Defense and Fire Support

The most common failure state in Ebbtide base building is a single-layer defense. One wall. One gate. One squad watching one approach. It works until it does not, and then it fails all at once.

Think in layers instead. Each layer has a job, and each layer should be cheap enough that losing it does not end your run.

| Layer | Job | Typical tools | Failure symptom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outer screen | Slow and channel the horde | Barricades, wire, open kill zones | Horde reaches the wall at full speed |
| Main line | Kill density | Emplacements, entrenched squads | Ammo runs dry before the wave does |
| Inner fallback | Buy time to reorganize | Secondary positions, choke gates | Collapse cascades with no regroup point |
| Overhead | Cover dead zones and roofs | Elevated firing positions | Ground line gets flanked from above |
| Fire support | Break up clusters | Artillery, air assets | You shell your own perimeter |

That last row is not a joke. Indirect fire is the most powerful tool in a game like this and the easiest way to delete your own squad. Community creators have highlighted artillery as a headline feature of Ebbtide base building, and the practical discipline is simple: call it on ground you are willing to lose, and never on ground your infantry is currently standing on.

A second discipline that pays off: pre-plan your fallback. Before a wave hits, walk your own base in your head and ask where you go if the main line breaks. If the answer is "somewhere, probably," you do not have a fallback — you have a panic.

## Coordinating Squads, Armor, and Air With Your Base

A base that never moves is a base that gets surrounded. The strength of Ebbtide base building is that it enables mobility rather than replacing it. Your structures exist to launch squads, repair vehicles, and hold ground you have already cleared.

| Asset | Best used for | Works well with | Risk if misused |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infantry squads | Holding chokes, escorting civilians | Emplacements and overlapping fire | Isolated and overwhelmed |
| Main battle tanks | Breaking dense clusters | Open lanes and infantry screening | Getting bogged down in tight streets |
| Helicopters | Reconnaissance and rapid response | Rooftop positions and forward bases | Flying blind into unknown density |
| Artillery | Reshaping a wave before contact | Pre-planned kill zones | Friendly casualties and wasted shells |

### Practical tips that hold up in almost any build

- **Build the exit before the entrance.** Vehicle lanes and fallback routes should exist before you add another wall.
- **Keep one squad mobile at all times.** Static defense with no reserve is a countdown timer.
- **Escort evacuations with structure coverage.** Civilian routes that pass under your guns are far safer than routes that pass beside them.
- **Rotate armor back for repairs early.** A tank at half health that retreats is worth more than a tank that dies heroically.
- **Watch where the horde actually comes from.** If ninety percent of waves arrive on one axis, stop fortifying the other three.

Community reports consistently frame Ebbtide as a game about managing attention — you cannot personally aim every rifle and also manage a base economy. The players who will do well are the ones who build systems that function without them for thirty seconds at a time.

## FAQ

**Is Ebbtide base building mandatory, or can I just play as a survivor?**
Based on what has been shown, the two halves are meant to support each other. You can absolutely spend most of a fight on the ground as a single survivor, but the base layer is what keeps squads supplied and gives armor somewhere to fall back to. Ignoring it entirely will likely make the later, larger waves much harder.

**Can I build anywhere in Los Angeles, or only at fixed strongholds?**
The developers have not published detailed placement rules yet. Footage emphasizes forward operating bases and rooftop positions, which suggests site choice matters a great deal. Until official rules are confirmed, assume terrain and elevation will drive where Ebbtide base building actually works.

**How does Ebbtide base building connect to civilian evacuations?**
Evacuation routes need protection, and protection comes from structures and overlapping fire. In practice, your base layout is an evacuation plan: routes that run through covered ground succeed far more often than routes that run past your perimeter.

**Is Ebbtide out yet?**
No. The game is still in development, and the studio has been clear that features and visuals shown in early footage may change. If you want to track availability, keep an eye on the [Ebbtide listings on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/search/?term=Ebbtide) and the developers' official channels rather than relying on reposted clips.

Ebbtide base building is ultimately a patience mechanic dressed up as a construction menu. The players who scout their ground, layer their defenses, and keep something in reserve will hold Los Angeles far longer than the ones who build one big wall and hope.
