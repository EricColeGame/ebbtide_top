## What Ebbtide RTS Gameplay Actually Looks Like

Most zombie games ask you to pick a lane. You either get a top-down strategy game where units are abstract counters, or a hands-on survival game where you personally feel every reload. Ebbtide is being pitched as both at once, and community footage shared by creators such as Pratama Gaming backs that up with clips of overhead squad orders followed by close-up combat on the streets of Los Angeles.

The elevator pitch: hold a major American city against massive zombie hordes using infantry squads, main battle tanks, helicopters, and callable artillery, while also stepping into the boots of a survivor on the ground. Because the project is still in development, treat every detail here as a snapshot rather than a final feature list.

| Pillar | What it means in play | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Hands-on survival | You control a survivor directly during fights | Keeps tension personal instead of abstract |
| Squad command | Order infantry units from an overhead view | Lets you cover more of the map than one body allows |
| Base building | Establish forward operating bases and defensive positions | Turns temporary ground into something worth defending |
| Combined arms | Tanks, helicopters, and artillery support | Gives you answers to horde density and spread |
| Evacuation objectives | Escort and protect civilians | Adds a moral and tactical cost to every push |

## The Two Layers of Ebbtide RTS Gameplay

The defining mechanic in Ebbtide RTS gameplay is the switch between commander view and survivor view. In commander view you are reading the whole battlefield — where the horde is massing, which rooftop is about to be overrun, which squad needs to fall back. In survivor view you are dealing with a single street corner, and the game becomes about positioning, ammo, and not getting surrounded.

Community reports suggest the real skill ceiling sits in the transition between those two modes. Players who stay in the commander view too long lose track of what their own avatar is doing. Players who stay on the ground lose the map. The footage makes it clear that both layers are meant to be load-bearing, not cosmetic.

| Layer | Camera and control | Best used for | Failure state |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commander view | Overhead tactical view, order-based | Reinforcing lines, calling artillery, repositioning armor | Getting flanked because you stopped watching the ground |
| Survivor view | Direct third-person control | Clearing interiors, plugging a breach, escorting civilians | Getting isolated from your own squads |
| Hybrid play | Constant switching | Pushing an advantage after a successful artillery strike | Losing the thread on either layer |

## Base Building, Rooftops, and Holding Los Angeles

Base building is where Ebbtide RTS gameplay becomes a map-control problem rather than a pure combat problem. The development footage leans hard on verticality — rooftop defenses, elevated firing positions, and forward operating bases that push your spawn and resupply point deeper into the city. In a city map, height is force multiplication: a small squad on a roof can hold a street that would swallow a platoon on the pavement.

The civilian evacuation angle adds a second job on top of all that. You are not just killing zombies; you are buying time and space for people who cannot fight back, which forces you to defend routes instead of just chokepoints.

| Defensive element | Function | Tactical note |
|---|---|---|
| Rooftop positions | Elevated fire and overwatch | Excellent against crowds, weak against air or artillery-level threats |
| Forward operating bases | Extend your reach into new districts | Each new base is a new thing the horde can attack |
| Evacuation corridors | Civilian escort objectives | Requires mobile defenders, not static turrets |
| Artillery support | Area denial and horde thinning | Best used before contact, not after |
| Armored vehicles | Mobile hardpoints | Strong in open streets, exposed in tight alleys |

## Combined Arms: Squads, Tanks, and Helicopters

The centerpiece of every Ebbtide RTS gameplay clip so far is scale. Community footage has shown main battle tanks — the Abrams-style armor people keep pointing at — grinding through enormous zombie crowds while helicopters circle overhead. That is a very different fantasy from the usual "small group of survivors in a hallway" zombie game, and it changes what your units are actually for.

| Unit | Role | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infantry squads | Line holding and clearing | Flexible, cheap to reposition, can enter buildings | Breaks under sustained pressure without support |
| Main battle tanks | Shock and crowd control | Enormous sustained damage, soaks attention | Slow, loud, and easy to surround in dense streets |
| Helicopters | Recon and rapid response | Vision and mobility across districts | Cannot hold ground on their own |
| Artillery | Pre-engagement shaping | Deletes density before it reaches you | Cooldown and timing dependent |
| Survivor | Precision and objectives | Handles interiors and escort details | One bad engagement ends the run |

## Practical Tips for Ebbtide RTS Gameplay

Everything below is built from what the footage demonstrates plus general RTS fundamentals. Since the game is unreleased, treat these as habits to bring in on day one rather than confirmed patch-specific strategy.

1. **Scout with mobility, not with infantry.** Helicopters and fast units should find the horde before your line does.
2. **Spend artillery early.** Waiting until the horde is already touching your defenses wastes the one tool that stops density.
3. **Build forward, but not greedily.** A forward operating base extends your reach and your vulnerability in the same move.
4. **Keep one squad in reserve.** The classic RTS mistake is committing everything and having nothing left to plug the breach.
5. **Use rooftops as force multipliers.** Two squads elevated can outperform six on the street.
6. **Treat evacuation routes as combat objectives.** Escorts fail when you defend the destination instead of the road.
7. **Switch views on a rhythm.** Check the map, act locally, check the map again — don't wait for something to go wrong.

| Common mistake | Immediate fix | Long-term payoff |
|---|---|---|
| Over-investing in one forward base | Cap yourself at one new base per push | Fewer catastrophic losses |
| Ignoring verticality | Put at least one squad on elevated ground per district | Cheaper defense with fewer casualties |
| Hoarding artillery | Fire on the leading edge of a horde | Fewer emergency scrambles |
| Staying in one camera mode | Alternate on a fixed timer | Better awareness at both scales |

## FAQ

**Is Ebbtide RTS gameplay more strategy or more shooter?**
Based on the footage, it is genuinely both. The commander layer handles positioning, reinforcement, and fire support, while the survivor layer handles precision work like interiors and escorts. Players who only engage with one half will find the other half punishing them.

**Can you play Ebbtide solo?**
Nothing in the material suggests a mandatory co-op requirement. The pitch is built around you commanding forces and fighting as a survivor, which reads as a single-player command fantasy first.

**When does Ebbtide release, and is there a confirmed date?**
No confirmed release window has been announced. The project is explicitly described as in development, and the creators sharing footage note that features and visuals may change. Follow official developer channels for updates rather than relying on reposted clips.

**What should I practice before Ebbtide RTS gameplay launches?**
Work on camera discipline and unit preservation in any squad-based RTS. The two transferable skills are scouting before committing and always keeping a reserve — both of which decide whether a horde defense holds or folds.
