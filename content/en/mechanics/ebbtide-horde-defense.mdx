## Why Ebbtide Horde Defense Is the Heart of the Game

Ebbtide horde defense is where the game's ambition stops being a pitch and becomes a stress test: a wall of infected pressing your lines while you juggle squads, armor, and a city that will not stop screaming. Because Ebbtide blends real-time strategy command with hands-on survival, every Ebbtide horde defense asks two questions at once — how well did you prepare before contact, and how fast can you adapt when that preparation falls apart? Get the answer right and Los Angeles holds another night. Get it wrong and you watch a forward operating base fold in real time.

Ebbtide is a zombie survival RTS currently in development. Publicly shown material emphasizes squad-based combat, base building, and large-scale survival, with the option to fight directly as a survivor or to command squads, armored vehicles, and helicopters. That combination is exactly why horde defense matters more here than in a pure strategy game: you are never only a general and never only a rifleman.

## What "Horde Defense" Actually Means in Ebbtide

In a traditional RTS, defense is a build order and a wall. In Ebbtide, defense is a **loop** — fortify, absorb the wave, evacuate who you can, then push back out before the next surge. Community footage suggests the scale is deliberately lopsided: enormous zombie counts against a limited number of trained survivors and expensive vehicles. That means your defense is less about killing everything and more about controlling where the killing happens.

The layered structure shown so far looks roughly like this:

| Layer | Job in the defense | Ebbtide elements seen so far |
|---|---|---|
| Rooftop overwatch | Elevation, sight lines, safe firing angles | Rooftop defenses, helicopter support |
| Forward operating base | Resupply, staging, respawn pressure | Base building, forward operating bases |
| Mobile response | Plugging breaches fast | Armored vehicles, squad orders |
| Indirect fire | Deleting a cluster before it reaches the line | Called-in artillery |
| Civilian corridor | Protecting non-combatants and keeping routes open | Civilian evacuations |

Notice that only two of those five layers are actually about damage. The rest are about **space, timing, and people**. A defense that only thinks about damage output usually collapses the moment a wave arrives from an angle it did not expect.

## The Three Phases of an Ebbtide Horde Defense

Every engagement breaks into three phases, and each one punishes a different kind of laziness.

### Phase 1: Before contact

This is where most horde defense runs are actually won. You are placing structures, choosing fallback positions, and deciding which corridors you are willing to give up. If a rooftop position has no route back to the base, it is not a strongpoint — it is a trap. Ask of every position: can the squad inside it retreat, or does it die there?

### Phase 2: During contact

Mid-wave, your job is triage. Something will always be bleeding. The discipline is deciding which loss is acceptable — a rooftop position, a vehicle, a stretch of street — and committing to that decision instead of splitting attention across five emergencies. Direct control is powerful here: taking over a survivor lets you personally stabilize a breach that squads would mishandle. But it also removes your attention from the map, which is how a second breach opens unnoticed.

### Phase 3: After the wave

The temptation is to spend the lull rebuilding everything exactly as it was. That is usually wrong. Rebuild what earned its keep, abandon what did not, and use the gap to reposition before the next surge. Repairing a structure that dies every wave is a slow way to lose.

| Phase | Primary goal | Lock this in first | Classic failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before contact | Shape the battlefield | Fallback routes and sight lines | Overextended forward positions |
| During contact | Triage losses | One priority breach at a time | Splitting attention everywhere |
| After the wave | Rebuild selectively | Reposition, don't just repair | Rebuilding the same broken layout |

## Units and Assets That Carry a Defense

The footage circulating so far leans heavily on combined arms — infantry squads alongside heavy armor. Treat the following as a framework for thinking about roles rather than a confirmed balance sheet, since the game is still in development.

| Asset | Strength | Limitation | Best paired with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Survivor squads | Flexible, cheap to reposition, hold interiors | Break under sustained pressure | Rooftop overwatch and armor support |
| Armored vehicles | Anchor a street, absorb the front of a wave | Poor in tight spaces, high value to lose | Infantry screening the flanks |
| Helicopters | Reach breaches fast, strong on open ground | Exposed and hard to replace | Ground squads to finish the fight |
| Artillery | Clears a cluster before it makes contact | Requires timing and a clear line | Anything that slows the horde down |
| Base structures | Sustain the whole operation | Static, and a magnet for pressure | Mobile response units nearby |

The core insight for any Ebbtide horde defense: **vehicles buy time, infantry holds space, artillery deletes problems.** If you are using armor to do infantry work, you will run out of armor.

## A Practical Ebbtide Horde Defense Checklist

Use this as a pre-wave routine. It is deliberately ordered by consequence rather than by cost.

| Priority | Action | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Confirm every position has a retreat route | Encircled units are lost units |
| 2 | Assign one squad as mobile reserve | Breaches need a responder, not a committee |
| 3 | Pre-plan artillery targets on approach lanes | You will not have time to aim mid-surge |
| 4 | Stage vehicles where they can reach two fronts | Static armor defends one street only |
| 5 | Open and mark civilian evacuation routes | Panic at the corridor costs more than zombies do |
| 6 | Set a clear "abandon" line for forward positions | Prevents slow, piecemeal losses |
| 7 | Bank resources for post-wave repairs | Waves end; the next one does not wait |

Two habits separate clean defenses from messy ones. First, **rehearse the collapse** — walk your own fallback routes mentally before you need them. Second, **never defend everything**. A short, dense line that holds beats a long, thin line that fails everywhere at once.

## Mistakes That Break an Ebbtide Horde Defense

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Spreading units evenly | Nothing is strong enough to matter anywhere | Concentrate, then cover gaps with mobility |
| Staying in direct control too long | The map goes unmanaged while you shoot | Set a timer and return to command view |
| Spending everything on one superweapon | One loss ends the defense | Diversify across layers |
| Ignoring evacuation pressure | Civilian losses compound into bigger problems | Treat corridors as part of the defense |
| Rebuilding identical positions | The same weakness gets exploited twice | Rework the layout during the lull |
| Chasing stragglers | Pulls your line out of shape | Let the fast movers go; hold the line |

Community reports from early footage suggest that the horde's scale is the real difficulty lever — not any single enemy type. That reinforces the same lesson: the fight is about geometry and tempo, not about winning every individual exchange.

## FAQ

**Is Ebbtide horde defense the same as base building?**
No. Base building is the infrastructure that supports a defense, but the Ebbtide horde defense loop also includes squad positioning, vehicle staging, evacuation management, and called-in fire support. Bases sustain the fight; they do not win it alone.

**Can you fight the horde yourself instead of commanding?**
Yes — the game is presented as offering both direct survivor control and top-down squad and vehicle command. The practical skill is knowing when to switch: hands-on control for a collapsing breach, command view for everything else.

**Is Ebbtide released yet?**
As of the material gathered for this article, Ebbtide is in development and its features and visuals may change. There is no confirmed final balance or content list to plan around yet.

**What is the single biggest Ebbtide horde defense mistake?**
Defending evenly. Spreading units across the whole perimeter means no position is strong enough to stop a concentrated push. Concentrate your strength, keep a mobile reserve, and accept that some ground is meant to be given up.

**Do vehicles replace infantry in a defense?**
No. Armor absorbs the front of a wave and buys time, but it cannot hold interiors or cover tight angles. A successful Ebbtide horde defense pairs vehicles with infantry screening and artillery shaping the approach.
