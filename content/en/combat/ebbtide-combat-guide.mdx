Ebbtide drops you into a collapsing Los Angeles with a handful of units, a build menu, and a horizon that keeps filling with zombies. Holding your first position is easy; holding your tenth is not — and that gap is exactly what this Ebbtide combat guide is built to close. Below you'll find the fundamentals of base layout, unit composition, fire support timing, and wave defense that separate a clean hold from a total collapse, whether you're brand new to the game or already losing runs in the mid-game.

## Ebbtide Combat Basics: What the Game Actually Asks of You

Ebbtide is a zombie survival RTS, not a shooter. You never aim a rifle — you manage a battlefield. That single distinction shapes everything in this Ebbtide combat guide, because almost every mistake new commanders make comes from thinking like a soldier instead of a planner.

Three systems drive every engagement:

- **Infrastructure** — what you can build, repair, and replace under pressure.
- **Force composition** — which units you field and in what ratio.
- **Fire support** — off-map and air assets that buy you minutes when the line bends.

Runs fall apart when a player over-invests in one layer. A beautiful base with no mobile reserve dies to a flank. A giant tank blob with no production dies to attrition. A support-heavy army with no frontline dies before the second call-in lands.

The other thing to internalize early: zombies in Ebbtide are a *pressure system*, not a puzzle. You are never "done" clearing. Every minute you spend not improving your position is a minute the horde spends growing. Play with that in mind and the rest of this Ebbtide combat guide will read like a checklist rather than a lecture.

## Opening Priorities: Base Layout and the First Waves

Your opening determines how much of the map you get to keep. The goal is not a fortress — it's a *working* position: production running, a defensible perimeter, and at least two routes out so you can never be fully surrounded.

Community reports and developer showcases consistently show the same rhythm: establish a foothold, push forward, build a forward operating base, repeat. Sitting still is a losing strategy because the horde scales faster than a static defense does.

| Phase | Primary goal | What to prioritize | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | Secure a production foothold | Core infrastructure, a small flexible squad | Building wide before production is stable |
| Early pressure | Extend map control | Forward operating base, scouted routes | Defending everywhere at once |
| Mid-game | Absorb large waves | Armor, layered defenses, repair capacity | Spending all resources on static defenses |
| Late game | Dictate the fight | Mobile reserves, artillery, air support | Letting the army sit idle between waves |

A few layout rules worth repeating:

1. **Never put production on the frontline.** Lose one building and your reinforcement loop collapses.
2. **Build around chokepoints, not open ground.** Narrow approaches multiply the value of every unit you own.
3. **Keep one corridor open.** A fully sealed base is a base you can't reinforce or evacuate from.
4. **Spread critical structures.** One unlucky breach should not delete your entire economy.

## Unit Roles and Army Composition

The biggest mid-game jump most players make is realizing that Ebbtide is a combined-arms game. A pure-infantry army evaporates against mass. A pure-armor army gets outmaneuvered and bled dry. The answer is a mix that covers each other's weaknesses.

| Unit type | Primary job | Strong against | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infantry | Hold lines, screen, absorb contact | Isolated stragglers, light swarms | Being surrounded or left unsupported |
| Armor (e.g., M2 Abrams) | Break dense hordes, anchor pushes | Packed masses of zombies | Getting bogged down without infantry cover |
| Air (e.g., AH-6M Little Bird) | Plug gaps, react fast, hit flanks | Clusters, exposed approach lanes | Overcommitting into anti-air or swarms |
| Artillery / fire support | Soften waves before contact | Large slow formations | Firing too late to matter |
| Engineers / support | Repair, rebuild, sustain | Nothing directly | Being forgotten until something breaks |

A practical rule of thumb: build your army as a **core plus a reaction force**. The core holds the line and does not chase. The reaction force is small, fast, and expendable — it responds to breaches, flanks, and unexpected pressure. If your entire army is the core, you have nobody to send when something goes wrong, and something always goes wrong.

Also, keep a production buffer. If you can't replace losses within a wave, you aren't defending — you're spending down a finite stockpile.

## Air Support and Fire Missions: When to Spend Them

Fire support is the most misused tool in the game. Players either hoard it until it's irrelevant or burn it on the first contact. Developer showcases have highlighted close air support like the AH-6M Little Bird lifting off from a flight deck, alongside armor columns grinding into enormous hordes — a clear signal that Ebbtide is designed around **layered** firepower rather than a single wonder-weapon.

| Support asset | Best used for | Timing | Risk if misused |
|---|---|---|---|
| Close air support | Sealing a breach or breaking a flank | Mid-engagement, once the horde commits | Wasted on a wave your line already handles |
| Artillery | Pre-softening a large approaching wave | Just before contact | Fired too early and wasted on scattered units |
| Armor push | Punching through a stalled front | When your infantry has fixed the enemy | Sent in alone with no screen |
| Repair / rebuild | Restoring a collapsed section | Immediately after a breach | Delayed until the next wave arrives |

Three habits that make support assets pay off:

- **Spend on the wave that threatens your production, not the wave that annoys your frontline.**
- **Pair every call-in with a ground plan.** Air support buys time; it doesn't hold ground.
- **Keep one asset in reserve.** The wave that kills you is almost always the one you had nothing left for.

## Wave Defense: Building a Defense That Scales

Wave defense in Ebbtide is about depth, not thickness. A single heavily defended point is a single point of failure. Three shallower layers that overlap are far more resilient, because a breach in one layer is still covered by the next.

| Threat pattern | Tell | Counter | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mass frontal push | Dense formation on a main road | Artillery + armor anchor | Concentrated fire into a concentrated target |
| Wide flanking spread | Units appearing on secondary routes | Mobile reaction force | Speed beats mass on open ground |
| Sustained attrition | Constant trickle, no break | Rotating infantry + repair | Prevents one section from collapsing |
| Breach behind the line | Spawns or leaks near production | Pre-positioned reserve | Fixes the problem before it compounds |

Practical defense tips:

- **Rotate your frontline.** Units that survive a wave should fall back and repair, not sit in place waiting to die.
- **Pre-place reserves before you need them.** Moving a unit mid-crisis costs time you don't have.
- **Watch the map, not the fight.** Zooming in on one skirmish is how players miss the flank that ends the run.
- **Rebuild immediately after a breach.** A hole in your line is an invitation for the next wave.

## Common Mistakes That Wreck Runs

Every failed run in this Ebbtide combat guide's hall of shame tends to fall into a handful of repeatable categories. Learn to spot the symptom and the fix becomes obvious.

| Mistake | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Static turtling | Waves keep getting bigger, your line stays the same size | Push out and build forward operating bases |
| No mobile reserve | One breach cascades into total collapse | Keep a small fast force uncommitted |
| Hoarding fire support | Sitting on unused call-ins all game | Spend on the wave that threatens production |
| Single-unit-type army | Hard counter ends the run instantly | Field a combined-arms mix |
| Ignoring economy | Can't replace losses after a big wave | Balance production with combat spending |
| Fighting every skirmish | Army is always out of position | Trade ground for time when needed |

One more mistake that doesn't fit neatly into a table: **playing without a plan for the next wave.** Ebbtide rewards players who think one wave ahead. Before the current fight ends, decide where your next base goes, which units you're rebuilding, and what support asset you're holding. That habit alone will carry you further than any micro trick.

## FAQ

**Is Ebbtide a shooter or a strategy game?**
It's a zombie survival RTS. You command units, build infrastructure, and call in support rather than controlling a single soldier. That's why every Ebbtide combat guide — including this one — focuses on positioning, composition, and resource timing instead of aim.

**What's the most important thing to learn first?**
Combined arms. New players who field a balanced mix of infantry, armor, and support survive noticeably longer than those who mass a single unit type. Add a small reaction force and you'll clear early waves comfortably.

**How should I use air support like the Little Bird?**
Treat it as a gap-closer, not a win button. Send it where your line is actually bending, and always pair it with ground units that can hold the space it clears. Keep one asset in reserve for emergencies.

**Where can I follow development updates?**
The official channel is [Ebbtide's developer updates on X](https://x.com/ProjectAliveDev), where the team posts showcases of units and systems in action. Community reports and player experience threads are also useful for seeing how other commanders approach wave defense.

Master the fundamentals above — layered defense, combined arms, disciplined fire support — and the horde stops being a wall you're surviving and starts being a problem you're solving.
