| Unit / System | Battlefield Role | Best Used For | Watch Out For |

The important takeaway: **Ebbtide weapons** are a system, not a shopping list. A tank that's screening for artillery is worth more than a tank fighting alone.

## Artillery in Ebbtide: Timing, Spotters, and Splash

Artillery is the most misunderstood tool in the game. New commanders treat it as a panic button — something you press when the line breaks. Experienced players treat it as a *scheduling* tool: something you use to delete a problem thirty seconds before it becomes a problem.

The showcase clip frames artillery as part of a layered defense, alongside base construction and armored pushes. That's the correct mental model. Here's how to think about fire support roles rather than specific shell types, since balance and availability shift as the game develops.

| Fire Support Job | What It Solves | When to Call It | Risk If You Get It Wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-contact shaping | Thins a horde before it reaches your line | While the wave is still forming up | Wasted shells on targets that scatter |
| Breach denial | Stops a gap from becoming a rout | The moment a line segment fails | Firing into your own retreating units |
| Chokepoint saturation | Maximizes damage in tight terrain | When enemies are funneled by buildings | Shells spent on empty streets |
| Emergency close support | Saves an isolated asset | Last resort, when a tank is about to die | Friendly-fire losses that cost more than the tank |

### Reading the Call-In Window

Artillery has travel time. That's the whole skill test. If you call fire on a horde's *current* position, you're hitting where they were. Aim where they'll be — the street they're being funneled into, the intersection behind the pack, the alley your retreating squad will need clear.

A practical habit: whenever you see a large mass forming, place your fire mission one beat ahead of it, then let your tanks pull the horde *into* the impact zone. You're not reacting to the wave. You're steering it.

## Forward Operating Bases: The Backbone of Your Guns

You cannot sustain **Ebbtide weapons and artillery** without a base plan. Forward operating bases are how you project force across the map instead of turtling in a corner, and they're how damaged armor gets back into the fight instead of sitting idle.

Player experience suggests the biggest base-building mistake is treating FOBs as purely defensive structures. They're not bunkers — they're logistics nodes that extend the reach of your artillery and armor.

| Priority | Structure Focus | Why It Matters | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Core base security | Protects your entire production and support chain | Immediately |
| 2 | Repair and resupply access | Returns damaged tanks to the line faster | Before your first armor loss |
| 3 | Forward observation | Improves targeting and reaction time for fire support | Once your line stabilizes |
| 4 | Advanced fire support assets | Unlocks heavier, longer-reaching options | After economy is safe |
| 5 | Redundant fallback nodes | Keeps you fighting if the front collapses | Before the first big wave |

The rule of thumb: build forward, but never build forward of what you can defend. An FOB that gets overrun is worse than no FOB at all — you lose the structure, the units garrisoning it, and the tempo.

## Tactical Playbook: Layering Ebbtide Weapons and Artillery

Here's a repeatable sequence that works across most defensive situations in Ebbtide. Treat it as a template, not a script.

| Step | Action | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify the funnel | Force the horde into terrain you've already ranged |
| 2 | Place pre-emptive fire missions | Break density before contact |
| 3 | Screen tanks with infantry | Stop flanking swarms from reaching armor |
| 4 | Commit armor to the densest cluster | Convert artillery disruption into kills |
| 5 | Rotate damaged units to the FOB | Sustain combat power across waves |
| 6 | Rebuild the line immediately | Never let a gap exist between waves |

A few actionable tips that come up repeatedly in community discussion:

- **Never let armor fight alone.** The most-liked comment on the showcase clip was blunt: if a zombie can damage a tank, the game has a problem. Whether or not that balance lands where players want, the practical lesson stands — protect your armor with screening infantry so it isn't taking chip damage from every angle.
- **Stagger your fire missions.** Two half-sized missions at different times usually beat one big alpha strike, because the second one catches the survivors bunching up again.
- **Keep one fire mission in reserve.** If you spend every shell on the visible wave, you have nothing for the breach you didn't see coming.
- **Watch the terrain, not the health bars.** Zombie hordes telegraph their path. Your artillery should already be ranged on that path before the wave commits.

## Mistakes That Get Your Line Broken

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | Fix |

| Calling fire too close | Friendly-fire losses erase your advantage | Pull units back before the impact window |

The through-line is tempo. Every one of these mistakes costs you time, and time is what **Ebbtide weapons and artillery** are actually buying you — time to reposition, time to repair, time to rebuild the line before the next wave arrives.

## FAQ

**What are the main Ebbtide weapons and artillery options?**
The arsenal is built around main battle tanks as the armored anchor, infantry and static defenses for line holding, and called-in fire support to shape hordes before they reach your positions. Exact availability depends on your base upgrades and current build.

**Is artillery or armor more important in Ebbtide?**
Neither wins alone. Armor holds ground; artillery makes holding it cheaper. The strongest defenses layer both, using fire support to thin hordes and tanks to finish what's left.

**How do I stop zombies from damaging my tanks?**
Screen your armor with infantry, keep tanks out of open ground where swarms can surround them, and pull damaged units back to a forward operating base for repair rather than letting them grind down.

**Do I need forward operating bases to use Ebbtide weapons and artillery effectively?**
In practice, yes. FOBs extend your reach, speed up repairs and resupply, and give your fire support better reach into the map. A defense without sustainment eventually collapses under its own attrition.
