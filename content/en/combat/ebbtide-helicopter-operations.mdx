## Formation Flying and Why Spacing Changes Everything

Formation flying is where Ebbtide helicopter operations get genuinely tactical. A tight formation looks cinematic and keeps aircraft mutually supporting, but it also turns four helicopters into one big target. A loose formation survives area attacks better, yet it stretches your coverage and makes timing harder to manage.

The developer has not published a full formation menu, so the table below is a doctrine primer based on how real rotary-wing operations work — the kind of shapes that fit naturally into a system built around adjustable spacing.

| Formation | Strength | Weakness | Best used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Column (single file) | Simple to fly, easy to sequence drops | Predictable, easy to ambush | Narrow urban corridors, sequenced insertions |
| Line abreast | Maximum simultaneous fire and coverage | Wide footprint, hard to turn | Sweeping an open approach together |
| Echelon (staggered diagonal) | Good visibility, flexible turning | One flank is exposed | Crossing a city while staying ready to break off |
| Vee or wedge | Strong mutual support, clear leader | Center aircraft is the anchor and the target | Escorted transport runs into contested airspace |
| Staggered trail | Hardest to hit as a group | Weak concentration of force | Withdrawals and long transits |

### Adjustable Spacing Is the Setting That Decides Who Survives

Spacing is the quiet hero of the whole system. When aircraft are packed tight, a single well-placed threat can end the mission. When they are spread out, the flight absorbs losses and keeps functioning. The trade-off is coordination: loose formations take longer to regroup over a landing zone, and stragglers arrive late.

Practical guidance for when the system reaches your hands:

- **Tighten up over friendly ground.** Transit over secure territory is the time to close ranks and move fast.
- **Spread out on approach.** The final leg into a contested landing zone is where spacing pays for itself.
- **Loosen further when carrying full squads.** A downed transport costs you the aircraft *and* the fireteam inside it.
- **Re-tighten before the drop.** You want boots on the ground in a tight window, not trickling in over thirty seconds.

## Combat Tips for Mastering Ebbtide Helicopter Operations

Based on the urban setting and the emphasis on squad deployment, a few habits will likely separate strong players from dead ones. None of this is confirmed tuning — it is the kind of thinking the systems appear designed to reward.

- **Treat aircraft as transport, not gunships.** Nothing shown so far suggests helicopters are flying weapons platforms. Their job is to deliver force, not win the fight alone.
- **Never commit the whole flight to one landing zone.** Split drops create pressure from two directions and preserve assets if one run goes wrong.
- **Watch the vertical sightlines.** In a city, threats come from above as often as from the street. Rooftop positions overlooking your approach are the first thing to scout.
- **Budget time for the regroup.** A loose formation needs a moment to reconcentrate after the drop. Plan for it instead of discovering it mid-fight.

| Threat category | Warning sign | Counter |
|---|---|---|
| Elevated ambush | Rooftops along your approach with clear sightlines | Shift the flight path one block over |
| Concentrated ground fire | Multiple shooters converging on one aircraft | Spread spacing and vary altitude |
| Chokepoint trap | A landing zone with only one exit route | Pick a two-exit LZ or drop short and walk in |
| Late-arriving stragglers | Aircraft trailing the formation into the LZ | Tighten spacing just before the drop |
| Overcommitment | Every squad in one basket | Stagger insertions across two locations |
