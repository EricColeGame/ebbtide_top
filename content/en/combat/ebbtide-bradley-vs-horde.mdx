A horde in Ebbtide doesn't break against a line — it pours around it. That's why the **Ebbtide Bradley vs horde** matchup has become one of the most debated tactical puzzles in the community: an infantry fighting vehicle has the speed to plug gaps and the firepower to shred a crowd, but only if you treat it as a mobile anchor instead of a rolling bunker. ## Why the Bradley Matters More Than Its Gun

Ebbtide is a survival RTS about commanding forces, building forward operating bases, calling in artillery, and holding a city while waves keep coming. In that framework, the Bradley's value isn't raw damage — it's tempo. It moves faster than heavy armor, carries infantry into the fight, and keeps firing while it repositions.

That combination matters because zombie hordes rarely attack where your defense is strongest. They leak. They flank. They pile into the seam between two strongpoints. A tank kills what's in front of it; a Bradley can leave, drive to the seam, drop troops to plug it, and still contribute fire support on arrival.

Community reports consistently echo one theme: the moment armor becomes static, it becomes a liability. Players who park vehicles at a chokepoint and forget them usually lose those vehicles to accumulated chip damage rather than a single dramatic breach. Mobility is armor.

## Ebbtide Bradley vs Horde: Where Each Unit Fits

Before you commit a Bradley to a lane, know what the rest of your army is doing. The table below maps the broad roles in an Ebbtide horde defense so you can spot the gap your IFV should fill.

| Unit / Asset | Primary Job | Best Against | Main Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main battle tank (Abrams) | Break dense clusters, hold a hard point | Tight packs, armored specials | Slow to reposition, easy to surround |
| Infantry fighting vehicle (Bradley) | Plug gaps, escort infantry, sustained fire | Spread-out runners, flanking groups | Overconfidence — it is not a tank |
| Infantry squads | Screen, absorb, occupy rooftops | Isolated stragglers, interior spaces | Attrition if left unsupported |
| Artillery | Delete a problem before it arrives | Large, slow, visible waves | Cooldown windows and friendly fire |
| Forward operating base | Resupply, reinforce, extend reach | Nothing directly — it's infrastructure | Overextension without a garrison |

The practical takeaway: your Bradley is a **reaction unit**, not a wall. Walls are what tanks and fortifications are for. The IFV is what you send when the wall develops a hole.

## Positioning: The Four Phases of a Wave

Every horde fight in Ebbtide runs through roughly the same arc. Position your Bradley differently in each phase, because the threat profile changes dramatically from the first contact to the cleanup.

| Phase | What's Happening | Bradley Priority | Support Needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | First contact, light pressure | Hold behind the screen, conserve ammo | Infantry forward, spotting |
| Surge | Density peaks, multiple lanes active | Shift to the thinnest lane, fire on the move | Artillery pre-plotting |
| Breach | Something gets through | Plug the gap, drop troops, disengage early | Nearby squad to occupy the hole |
| Cleanup | Scattered runners | Hunt in pairs, never solo | Infantry sweep of interiors |

The most common failure point is the **breach** phase. Players commit the Bradley to kill what got through, then leave it sitting in the gap it just closed. That's exactly when a second wave arrives and the vehicle is out of position with no infantry around it. Close the hole, hand it to infantry, and pull back.

A second positioning rule that experienced players repeat: **never let the Bradley be the frontmost unit in an open lane**. It should be second or third in depth, firing over a screen. If zombies are reaching it from three sides, you've already lost the engagement — you're just watching it happen.

The one comment that keeps showing up under every armored Ebbtide clip — "if a zombie can damage a tank, the game sucks" — misses the design point. Attrition *is* the tension. Armor in Ebbtide isn't invulnerable; it's efficient. Your job is to make it efficient enough that the math works in your favor before the wave ends.

## Building the Support Net Around Your Armor

A Bradley with no base behind it is a countdown timer. The vehicle needs three things to stay in the fight: a forward operating base close enough to matter, artillery covering its fallback route, and infantry to occupy the ground it clears.

**Priority order for support investment:**

- **Forward operating base placement** — close enough to reinforce, far enough to survive a leak.
- **Artillery coverage** — aimed at the approach, not the objective. You want to hit waves before they arrive.
- **Infantry garrison** — bodies on the ground the Bradley can fall back behind.
- **Repair capacity** — rotating damaged vehicles out is cheaper than replacing them.

Community footage of Ebbtide's Los Angeles defense scenarios shows how fast the scale escalates. Waves don't arrive as a single blob — they arrive as pressure on multiple axes, which means your artillery can only be in one place. That's the gap a mobile IFV fills. Artillery deletes one problem; the Bradley buys time on the other three.

One practical habit worth building: **assign each vehicle a lane and a fallback point before the wave starts**. Mid-wave improvisation is how Bradleys end up isolated in the middle of a street with no infantry support and no retreat route.

## Five Mistakes That Kill Bradleys

| Mistake | What It Costs | The Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Using the Bradley as a tank | Wasted vehicle, lost lane | Keep it behind a screen |
| Parking at a chokepoint | Attrition death by a thousand cuts | Rotate positions between waves |
| Ignoring the flanks | A breach you never saw | Reposition on the first sign of spread |
| No infantry escort | No eyes, no occupation | Pair every IFV with a squad |

If you take one thing from this guide, take the first row. Almost every bad **Ebbtide Bradley vs horde** outcome traces back to a player expecting a fighting vehicle to behave like a fortress. It isn't one. It's the thing that makes the fortress work.

## FAQ

**Is the Bradley better than a tank against hordes in Ebbtide?**
No — they solve different problems. Tanks break dense clusters and hold hard points. The Bradley plugs gaps, escorts infantry, and repositions fast. A defense built on one or the other is weaker than a defense built on both.

**How many Bradleys should I bring to a big wave?**
Start with one per active lane and add a second only when you have the infantry and repair capacity to support it. Unsupported armor is a liability, not a multiplier. Adjust based on what the current build actually rewards.

**What's the biggest mistake in an Ebbtide Bradley vs horde fight?**
Stopping. The moment a Bradley becomes static it starts losing to accumulated damage. Keep it moving between firing positions, and always have a fallback route covered by artillery.

**Does artillery make the Bradley obsolete?**
Not at all. Artillery handles the wave you can predict. The Bradley handles the wave that goes somewhere you didn't expect — and in Ebbtide, there's always one of those.

Armor alone won't hold Los Angeles. Armor used with tempo, infantry screens, and a base that keeps it repaired just might.
