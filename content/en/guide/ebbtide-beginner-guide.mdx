## What Ebbtide Is and Why It Plays Differently

Ebbtide hands you a rifle in one hand and a command console in the other, then drops a zombie outbreak on top of Los Angeles. That double duty is the entire hook — and the reason so many first-time players freeze in their opening minutes. This Ebbtide beginner guide exists to remove that freeze by turning an ambitious genre mashup into a sequence of decisions you can actually follow.

Community coverage of the game describes squad-based combat, base building, and large-scale zombie survival layered into one package, with the option to fight on the ground as a survivor or pull back to direct squads, armored vehicles, and helicopters. Any Ebbtide beginner guide worth reading has to start there, because the mode you spend most of your time in shapes everything else.

One honest caveat before you plan your first run: Ebbtide is still in development, and the studio has said that features and visuals shown in previews may change. Learn the rhythm of the loop rather than memorizing a build order, and revisit your assumptions after each update.

| Element | What it means in play | Beginner priority |
|---|---|---|
| Direct survivor control | You personally fight, move, and react on the ground | High — this is where you learn threat patterns |
| Squad command | You issue orders to groups instead of one character | High — the core skill ceiling |
| Base building | Forward operating bases and defensive structures | Medium — needed before the first big wave |
| Armored vehicles | Heavy firepower and mobile cover | Medium — strong, but easy to lose |
| Air support | Helicopters for mobility and overwatch | Low early — expensive attention sink |
| Fire support | Artillery and similar call-ins | Situational — devastating when timed well |

## The Three Pillars Every Ebbtide Beginner Guide Should Cover

### Pillar 1: Toggling between boots and overhead view

New players tend to pick a favorite mode and stay there. Ground players get ambushed because they never look at the wider map; command players lose squads because they never see what a street actually looks like. The fix is a rhythm: fight on the ground during the sharp moments, pull up to command during the lulls.

| Mode | Best used for | Biggest risk |
|---|---|---|
| Direct control | Clearing tight spaces, confirming threats, quick reactions | Losing situational awareness of the whole map |
| Squad command | Coordinating multiple groups, holding lines, repositioning | Micromanaging one fight while another collapses |
| Hybrid (toggling) | Nearly everything after your first hour | Requires discipline and a habit of checking the map |

### Pillar 2: Building forward, not building pretty

Base building in Ebbtide is not decoration — it is how you convert ground into time. Forward operating bases push your reach outward; rooftop defenses let you hold vertical ground; artillery and evacuation points turn a static structure into a working machine. Build for function first, aesthetics later.

### Pillar 3: Treat the horde as pressure, not as a boss

Large zombie hordes are less like a single enemy and more like weather. You do not "kill" a wave so much as survive its peak and rebuild before the next one. That mindset shift is the single biggest difference between players who improve fast and players who keep losing the same way.

## Your First Session: A Step-by-Step Opening Plan

The plan below is deliberately general, since balance in an in-development game shifts constantly. Use it as an order of operations rather than a script.

| Phase | Goal | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Opening minutes | Learn your survivor's movement, aiming, and interaction timings | Sprinting toward every sound |
| First engagements | Fight small groups on the ground to learn enemy behavior | Chasing kills into open ground |
| First command pass | Assign squads to simple, static tasks | Complex multi-front orders |
| First base | Establish a forward operating base with one clear purpose | Sprawling structures with no defenders |
| First big wave | Hold a prepared position and use fire support once, well | Calling artillery on the first cluster you see |
| Post-wave | Repair, reposition, and scout before expanding | Immediately pushing into new territory |

Two principles carry you through all six phases. First, every expansion should answer a question — where is the next threat coming from, and what do I need to meet it? Second, never spend your last reserve of fire support on a problem infantry could solve. An Ebbtide beginner guide can give you a sequence, but only your own map reading tells you when to break it.

## Squad, Vehicle, and Fire Support Roles Explained

Combined arms is the heart of the game's combat identity. Creator footage has highlighted main battle tanks rolling into enormous zombie concentrations, which looks spectacular — and is exactly the kind of moment that teaches bad habits if you lead with armor every time.

| Asset | Strength | Weakness | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infantry squads | Flexible, cheap to reposition, holds ground | Fragile in the open, breaks under sustained pressure | Line holding, escorting, clearing interiors |
| Armored vehicles | Heavy firepower, mobile cover for infantry | Clumsy in tight streets, draws attention | Breaking up dense clusters, anchoring a push |
| Helicopters | Speed, reach, overhead information | High attention cost, vulnerable when unsupported | Repositioning, evacuations, spotting |
| Artillery and call-ins | Deletes a problem area on your terms | Long commitment, poor into scattered targets | Pre-planned kills on chokepoints |
| Static defenses | Always on, no attention required | Immobile, predictable | Covering retreat routes and rooftops |

The practical rule: infantry finds the fight, armor decides it, artillery ends it. If you invert that order, you waste the expensive tools on problems the cheap ones could have handled. Experienced players describe this as "spending attention, not ammunition" — the resource you are really managing is your own focus.

## Base Building: Layering Defenses That Do Not Collapse

A base that dies to the first breach was not a base; it was a speed bump. Layer your defenses so that each layer buys the next one time to work.

| Layer | Purpose | Beginner tip |
|---|---|---|
| Outer perimeter | Slows and channels the horde | Leave deliberate gaps you can cover, not random holes |
| Rooftop positions | Vertical fire and observation | Staff them before the wave, not during it |
| Fallback line | A second place to fight from | Pre-place supplies and a rally point |
| Logistics core | Keeps squads supplied and repaired | Keep it away from the perimeter |
| Evacuation route | Moves civilians and wounded out | Scout it on the ground at least once |

Civilian evacuations and rooftop defense are recurring themes in what has been shown of the game, and they pull in opposite directions: evacuation wants open routes, defense wants closed ones. Plan both before contact, because mid-wave is the worst possible time to discover your only exit is full of zombies.

## Common Beginner Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

| Mistake | Why it hurts | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Staying in one control mode | Blind spots in either tactics or strategy | Toggle on a timer — every couple of minutes, check the other view |
| Expanding too early | Splits defenders across unfinished positions | Finish one position before starting the next |
| Leading with armor | Tanks draw the densest crowds and get surrounded | Send infantry first, armor second |
| Hoarding fire support | The "perfect" moment never arrives | Set a threshold in advance and commit when it is met |
| Ignoring evacuation routes | Trapped civilians become a second crisis | Scout exits before every major engagement |
| Over-microing one squad | The rest of the line collapses unattended | Give standing orders, then trust them |

The most common thread in community reports is attention mismanagement rather than mechanical failure. Players rarely lose because they aimed badly; they lose because they were watching the wrong part of Los Angeles when the line broke.

## FAQ

**Is Ebbtide available to play right now?**
The game is currently in development, and the studio has noted that features and visuals shown in previews may change. Check official channels and storefront listings for the current state of play rather than assuming a release window.

**Do I need real-time strategy experience to enjoy it?**
No, but you do need to accept the toggle. Players coming from shooters usually pick up the ground combat quickly and struggle with squad orders; players coming from strategy games have the opposite problem. A good Ebbtide beginner guide should train both instincts, which is why this one starts with switching modes.

**Can I ignore base building and just fight?**
You can for a while, but the horde does not ignore your base. Even a minimal forward operating base with a fallback line and a staffed rooftop position buys you the time you need to keep fighting on your terms.

**What is the single most important tip for a first run?**
Protect your attention. Decide in advance what deserves your focus, give standing orders so squads can act without you, and commit your heavy support on a threshold instead of waiting for a perfect moment that never comes.
