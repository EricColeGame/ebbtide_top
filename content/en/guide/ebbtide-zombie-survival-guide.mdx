## The Two Ways to Play

The core hook of Ebbtide is that you can drop into the fight yourself or stay in the commander's chair. Neither mode is "correct," but they solve different problems, and knowing when to switch is the single biggest skill gap in the game.

| Playstyle | Strength | Weakness | Best Used When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hands-on survivor | Immediate reaction, precision on key targets | You lose map awareness while fighting | A breach is happening right now |
| Commander view | Full battlefield picture, coordinated pushes | Slower response to local surprises | Setting up defenses and waves |
| Hybrid switching | Fixes both problems | Requires discipline and hotkey comfort | Most of the match |

Community reports from early footage suggest the hybrid approach is the intended way to play. The practical rhythm looks like this:

- **Command first.** Place squads, set fallback positions, and queue up base construction before the wave arrives.
- **Fight second.** Drop into a survivor during the peak of an assault, when a single well-aimed weapon matters more than another order.
- **Pull back out.** The moment the wave thins, return to command view and reposition for the next push.

A useful habit: never enter direct control without first giving your other squads a standing order. An idle unit is a dead unit in any **Ebbtide zombie survival guide** worth its salt.

## Base Building Priorities

Base building is where the strategy layer earns its keep. You are not decorating a map — every wall, turret, and ammo cache is a bet on where the horde will hit next. Build for the wave you expect, not the wave you want.

| Structure Type | Priority | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Perimeter barricades | Critical | Buys time and channels enemies into kill zones |
| Elevated firing positions | Critical | Rooftops give sightlines and protect shooters |
| Forward operating base | High | Shortens the distance between resupply and the front |
| Choke-point fortifications | High | Turns a wide street into a manageable corridor |
| Fallback safehouse | Medium | Somewhere to regroup if the line collapses |
| Decoy or bait positions | Situational | Pulls hordes away from evacuation routes |

Three habits that separate strong builders from overwhelmed ones:

1. **Build in depth, not in a line.** A single wall always falls. Three layers of resistance, each with its own firing position, is what actually stops a horde.
2. **Never build where you cannot shoot.** A barricade without overlapping fire is just a delay tactic.
3. **Protect your logistics.** Forward bases and supply routes are force multipliers; lose them and your best squad becomes a stranded squad.

Rooftop defense deserves its own mention. Footage has emphasized elevated positions overlooking streets and evacuation corridors, and that is no accident — height converts a wide-open urban map into a series of manageable firing lanes.

## Squad Combat, Armor, and Fire Support

Ebbtide's combat layer is built on combined arms. Infantry holds ground, armor breaks masses, aircraft repositions and rescues, and artillery erases mistakes. Using one without the others is how runs end early.

| Asset | Best Used For | Watch Out For |
|---|---|---|
| Infantry squads | Holding choke points, escorting civilians | Getting surrounded without support |
| Armored vehicles | Breaking dense hordes, punching through | Getting isolated and swarmed |
| Helicopters | Rapid redeployment, evacuation cover | Overextending into uncleared airspace |
| Artillery | Deleting clusters before they reach the line | Firing too close to your own units |
| Survivor (you) | Killing priority targets and plugging gaps | Losing track of the wider battle |

Practical tips for each layer:

- **Infantry:** keep squads in mutually supporting pairs. One squad alone is a speed bump; two squads covering each other are a wall.
- **Armor:** never send a tank down a street without infantry nearby. Vehicles dominate open ground and die in ambushes.
- **Air:** treat helicopters as a rescue and repositioning tool first, a weapon second. Losing an aircraft to save one squad is usually a bad trade.
- **Artillery:** fire early, not late. A strike that lands before the horde reaches your barricades saves structures; one that lands after saves nothing.

This is the part of the **Ebbtide zombie survival guide** where player experience matters most. Community reports consistently flag overextension as the number one cause of collapse — players push a strong squad forward, lose it, and then have nothing left for the actual defense.

## Holding Los Angeles: A Phase-by-Phase Defense Plan

Los Angeles is the battlefield, and it is a brutal one: dense streets, limited sightlines, and civilians scattered exactly where you least want them. Treat the defense as a sequence rather than a scramble.

| Phase | Goal | Key Actions |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Establish a foothold | Claim elevated positions, set initial barricades, scout approaches |
| Consolidation | Build depth | Add a second and third defensive layer, open a forward operating base |
| Escalation | Absorb the first big wave | Hold chokes, keep armor in reserve, use artillery on clusters |
| Evacuation | Move civilians safely | Clear and hold corridors, escort with infantry, cover with air |
| Attrition | Trade ground for time | Fall back in order, abandon what you cannot hold, preserve squads |

### Civilian Evacuation Is a Combat Objective

Evacuations are not side quests. Every civilian you move is a reason the defense continues, and every route you open becomes a road the horde can use too. Clear a corridor before you fill it, garrison the buildings along it, and keep at least one alternate route scouted at all times.

### Five Mistakes That Get Survivors Killed

- **Chasing kills.** The horde is not a score counter. Positioning beats body count every time.
- **Ignoring the flanks.** Zombies do not respect your front line; check the sides of every corridor.
- **Hoardng artillery.** Saving a strike for a "better moment" usually means never firing it.
- **Abandoning command view.** If you have not looked at the map in 60 seconds, you are already behind.
- **Defending everything.** You cannot hold every street. Pick the ground that protects your evacuation routes and let the rest go.

That last point is the heart of any good **Ebbtide zombie survival guide**: the game rewards players who choose where to fight, not players who try to fight everywhere. ## FAQ

**Is this Ebbtide zombie survival guide based on a finished game?**
No. Ebbtide is still in development, and the studio has stated that features and visuals may change. This guide is built from revealed gameplay footage and community reports, so treat specific mechanics as directional rather than final.

**Can I play Ebbtide as a pure RTS, or do I have to fight on the ground?**
Based on what has been shown, both approaches are supported. You can command squads, vehicles, and aircraft from an overhead view, or take direct control of a survivor. Most players will get the best results by switching between the two.

**What is the most important thing to build first in Ebbtide?**
Elevated firing positions and layered barricades. Height gives you sightlines, and depth gives you time — the two resources that decide whether a defense holds. Forward operating bases come next, once your core line is stable.

**How important is artillery in the Ebbtide zombie survival guide meta?**
Fire support appears to be a decisive tool for thinning large hordes before they contact your defenses. The key is timing: use it on approach, not after the horde has already reached your barricades. Pair it with infantry spotting so you are not wasting shells on empty streets.
