## The Ebbtide Survival Guide Mindset: Read First, Commit Second

The single biggest cause of early collapse in tactics games is committing before you understand the board. Units move into fog, resources get spent on the wrong upgrade, and a retreat that should have happened two turns ago becomes a wipe.

An **Ebbtide survival guide** that ignores this is just a list of tips. So build your run around a three-beat rhythm: read, commit, recover.

| Phase | What You Do | What It Protects |
|---|---|---|
| Read | Identify terrain, chokepoints, and enemy reach before moving anyone | Prevents turn-one overextension |
| Commit | Spend resources only on the axis you have already scouted | Prevents split focus and wasted upgrades |
| Recover | Pull back damaged units and reset formation | Preserves veterans and tempo |

Two habits make this work in practice.

- **Count enemy reach every turn.** Before you move a unit, ask which enemies could touch that tile next turn. If the answer is more than one, you are probably trading badly.
- **Set a loss threshold in advance.** Decide what you are willing to lose before a fight starts. Runs end when players keep "just one more turn" pushing into a bad engagement.

Community reports from tactics players consistently echo the same thing: the runs that last are the boring ones, where the player retreats early and re-engages on better ground.

## Unit Roles and Resource Discipline That Keep Runs Alive

Ebbtide is built around units and battlefields, which means role clarity matters more than raw stats. Most squad tactics games settle into a small set of archetypes, and knowing them lets you evaluate any roster quickly once the full lineup is revealed.

| Archetype | Job on the Board | Common Failure |
|---|---|---|
| Anchor / frontline | Holds chokepoints, absorbs first contact | Pushed too far forward without support |
| Ranged / skirmisher | Punishes overextension from safety | Left exposed after repositioning |
| Support | Repairs, buffs, or repositions allies | Treated as a second combat unit |
| Scout | Reveals terrain and enemy layout | Sacrificed for information too early |
| Flex | Fills gaps in a formation | Deployed without a defined job |

Three resource rules carry across almost every tactics game, and they belong in any **Ebbtide survival guide**:

1. **Never upgrade a unit you are about to lose.** Repair, reposition, or replace first. Upgrades compound; a dead unit's upgrades do not.
2. **Keep a reserve.** If every unit is committed, you have no answer to a surprise. A single uncommitted unit is often worth more than a stat boost.
3. **Value information over damage early.** Knowing where the enemy is beats killing one of them in the first exchange, because the knowledge applies to every turn after.

One more discipline point: resist the urge to build a "perfect" squad on paper. In practice, squads that survive are the ones with redundancy — two units that can do the same job, so losing one is survivable.
