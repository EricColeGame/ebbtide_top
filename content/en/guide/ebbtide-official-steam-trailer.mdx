### The Map Really Is the Battlefield

The single most important thing the Ebbtide official Steam trailer communicates is the absence of a mission list. There are no discrete levels and no hidden auto-resolve ticking away in the background. Scroll out and the fight keeps running; scroll in and you are looking at the same soldiers, the same wall, the same burning building. Every casualty carries back to the strategic layer.

That design choice has a consequence the trailer implies but does not spell out: you cannot win everywhere. Fifteen districts will fall out of Hold status, and the game is explicitly built around triage — deciding who you pull out first rather than saving everyone.

### A Horde With Actual Behavior

The trailer's second hook is that the infected are not scenery. They pile up to scale walls and rooftops, and they grab at helicopters hovering too low. Soldiers killed by the horde get back up on the other side. That last detail changes how you value a defensive line: a lost position does not just cost you ground, it feeds the thing attacking you.

### Direct Control and Real Firepower

Between the map layer and the horde layer sits a command layer. You can pause at any moment or run the operations map at up to 4x speed, issue orders to National Guard, LAPD, and LA County Sheriff units, call in Marines from Camp Pendleton, and take direct control when a fight needs a human hand.

The firepower reel is deliberately excessive — howitzers, rockets, cruise missiles, gunships — but the trailer includes a balancing beat: ship magazines are finite, and heavy weapons collapse buildings you may have wanted to keep.

## How to Read the Trailer Like a Strategy Player

Watching for spectacle is fine once. Watching for systems is what actually tells you whether Ebbtide is your kind of game.

- **Count the UI, not the explosions.** Pause on any frame with the map open and look for district status indicators and convoy icons. Those are the verbs you will spend most of your time using.
- **Note what is hidden.** The store page states that only hordes inside your forces' line of sight appear on the map. Fog of war at the strategic layer means scouting matters as much as shooting.
- **Watch the supply chain, not the kill count.** Soldiers, vehicles, and aircraft burn ammunition and fuel and can only rearm at bases you hold. Walls need building materials hauled in by hooklift truck or helicopter sling. The trailer shows convoys because convoys are the real game.
- **Treat the world campaign as the payoff.** What you evacuate through the Port of Los Angeles becomes your starting force aboard the carrier task force Ark when the campaign goes global. Early decisions compound for dozens of hours.
- **Check the accessibility notes.** Custom volume controls, stereo sound, and no mandatory QTEs are all listed — useful if you play with audio off or prefer pause-heavy strategy.
