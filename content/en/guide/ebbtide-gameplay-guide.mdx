## What Ebbtide Actually Is (and What It Isn't)

Ebbtide asks a question every zombie fan has argued about: do you fight the horde yourself, or command the battle from above? Based on the gameplay footage circulating online, the answer is that you do both — and knowing when to switch is the single biggest skill gap in the game. This Ebbtide gameplay guide breaks down the systems we've actually seen: squad commands, base building, armored vehicles, artillery support, and rooftop defense. If you'd rather be useful on day one than panicking on day one, treat this Ebbtide gameplay guide as your starting briefing.

Ebbtide is an in-development strategy-survival hybrid set in a collapsing Los Angeles. Instead of forcing you to choose between commanding an army and fighting on the ground, it hands you both: you can play as a single survivor in the streets, then pull back into a command view to direct squads, armored vehicles, and helicopters against enormous zombie hordes. Community footage also shows rooftop defenses, civilian evacuations, and artillery strikes called in on demand.

That combination is why the details matter. ## The Core Loop: How an Ebbtide Session Plays Out

Player experience suggests sessions follow a repeating rhythm rather than a single scripted campaign push: stabilize what you have, build up, expand your footprint, then survive the response. The pressure comes from the fact that building and fighting compete for the same attention — every second you spend in a menu is a second the horde spends walking toward your line.

| Phase | Typical focus | Priority actions |
|---|---|---|
| Stabilize | Secure your starting position | Set defensive arcs, identify chokepoints |
| Build | Establish production and defenses | Place your forward base with room to expand |
| Expand | Push into contested blocks | Screen vehicles with infantry, keep retreat routes open |
| Defend | Absorb a large horde push | Pre-position artillery, hold rooftops, rotate wounded squads |
| Recover | Rebuild losses | Replace destroyed assets before pushing again |

The phase most players will lose on is "defend," because it arrives while you're still mid-expansion. The fix is boring but effective: never start an expansion you can't afford to abandon. If a push collapses, falling back to a prepared line costs you ground; falling back to nothing costs you the run.

## Command Mode vs. Direct Control

The rest of this Ebbtide gameplay guide assumes you'll spend most of your time flipping between two very different mindsets. Command mode is about information and logistics. Direct control is about execution. Neither is "better" — the mistake is staying in one because it feels comfortable.

| Aspect | Command mode | Direct control |
|---|---|---|
| What you see | The whole battlefield | A narrow slice of it |
| Best at | Coordination, retreats, support calls | Holding a critical point, precise pushes |
| Weakness | Slow reaction to sudden breaches | Tunnel vision on one fight |
| Failure state | Ignoring a collapsing flank | Ignoring the other three flanks |
| Ideal moment | Before and between engagements | During a breach or a time-critical objective |

### When to take direct control

- A defensive line is about to break and needs one more gun.
- An evacuation objective needs an escort that won't wander off.
- A high-value target is exposed and your squads are out of position.

### When to stay in command view

- Multiple threats appear at once and priority has to be decided.
- Vehicles are taking damage and need to be pulled back before they're lost.
- Support assets are off cooldown and something worth hitting exists.

A useful habit from player experience: set a mental timer. If you've been in direct control for more than a short engagement, pull back out. The map does not pause for you, and the horde does not care that you're having a good fight.

## Base Building Priorities for Holding Los Angeles

Base building in Ebbtide isn't decoration — it's how you convert space into time. Every structure should answer a specific question: what does this let me hold that I couldn't hold otherwise?

| Build priority | Why it earns its slot | Risk if skipped |
|---|---|---|
| Defensive perimeter | Turns open ground into a kill zone | Squads get flanked and eaten piecemeal |
| Forward operating base | Shortens reinforcement distance | Every loss becomes a long walk back |
| Elevated firing positions | Rooftops multiply your effective force | Streets become a grind you can't win |
| Vehicle staging area | Keeps armor repaired and ready | Tanks arrive late or arrive broken |
| Fallback line | Gives you somewhere to retreat to | One breach becomes a total collapse |

Two practical rules worth internalizing:

1. **Build in depth, not in a line.** A single wall of defenses is a single point of failure. Layers buy you the seconds you need to respond.
2. **Never build where you can't retreat from.** Forward operating bases are aggressive tools. If the position has no exit, it's a trap with a nice name.

Community reports also suggest that civilian evacuation objectives will force you to defend something other than your own base. That changes the math: an evacuation route is a second frontline, and it needs its own screening force or it becomes a slow, expensive failure.

## Vehicles, Artillery, and Support Assets

Heavy assets are the most tempting thing in the game and the easiest to waste. A tank that dies alone in a side street is worse than no tank at all, because you paid for it and got nothing.

| Asset | Best used for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Main battle tanks | Breaking up dense horde clusters | Getting surrounded and immobilized |
| Helicopters | Repositioning, extraction, quick response | Being the only thing you're watching |
| Artillery | Pre-emptive strikes on known approach routes | Firing into areas your own squads occupy |
| Infantry squads | Screening armor and holding ground | Being spread so thin they can't mass fire |
| Rooftop teams | Sustained fire from relative safety | Losing access routes and getting cut off |

The pattern across all of them is the same: support assets amplify a plan, they don't replace one. If you don't know what you're trying to hold, the tank won't tell you. For a sense of how the broader survival-strategy space handles these tradeoffs, browsing [Steam's survival and strategy catalog](https://store.steampowered.com/) is a useful comparison exercise — you'll notice the games that endure are the ones where support tools have clear counters.

### Five habits that separate clean runs from messy ones

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Over-investing in one push | One bad engagement erases your whole army | Keep a reserve you never commit first |
| Ignoring rooftops | You fight the horde on its terms | Always hold at least one elevated position |
| Hoarding artillery | Damage unspent is damage wasted | Fire on approach routes, not on contact |
| Fighting in command view | You lose winnable local fights | Drop in when a line is about to break |
| Fighting in direct control | You lose the war while winning a street | Set a timer and pull out |

## FAQ

**Is there a release date for Ebbtide?**
No confirmed launch date has been announced. The project is still in development, and the studio has indicated that features and visuals shown in previews may change. Check the official channels before planning around any date you see in a comment section.

**Does this Ebbtide gameplay guide apply to the final version?**
Partly. The core tension — commanding an army versus fighting as one survivor — appears to be the design's foundation, so tactics around positioning, screening armor, and defending in depth should carry over. Specific numbers, unit balance, and build costs almost certainly won't.

**Can you win by only commanding squads and never fighting yourself?**
Community footage suggests pure command play is viable but harder, because some moments demand a body on a specific spot. Most successful play seems to involve short, decisive bursts of direct control followed by a return to the command layer.

**What should a new player learn first?**
Learn the switch. Before you memorize unit counters or build orders, practice moving between command view and direct control without losing track of the map. That one habit is worth more than any tier list, and it's the first thing this Ebbtide gameplay guide would put on a practice list.

**Is Ebbtide a strategy game or a survival game?**
It's genuinely both, and that's the appeal. The survival layer gives individual fights weight; the strategy layer gives them consequences. Players who enjoy one genre but resent the other will find the middle ground uncomfortable — and that's worth knowing before you commit.
